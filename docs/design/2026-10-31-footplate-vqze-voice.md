# Talk with the yard by voice in footplate

Implementation update (6 November 2026): ElevenLabs Agent is now an optional
provider alongside GPT-Live; its wire and refusal rules live in
`src/voice/elevenlabs.rs`. It reuses the existing Voice control, audio queue,
explicit-start gate and ordinary-mail path. Authentication, agent/model access,
expired URL, rate/concurrency and quota refusals stop the session without
reconnect, URL refresh, mail replay or provider/billing fallback. Only known
machine codes select local messages; provider prose and credentials stay out.
Connected time drives a call-only reference estimate ($0.08/min before discounts,
excluding burst pricing); pass-through LLM usage remains unknown. Settings
explains context growth, the silence discount and finite pending-work idle
exception. Account entitlement, audio saving, retention, training and residency
remain unverified until human commissioning. Two-year provider-default retention
is disclosed with separate audio/transcript controls; recommended controls do
not claim Enterprise ZRM. No local audio/transcript archive is added. See the
[Agent template and current behaviour](../window.md#elevenlabs-agent-template).


The single-provider choice below is historical. For the current provider
choices, key handling and Agent setup, see [Voice settings](../window.md#settings).

Design footplate-vqze, proposed 2026-10-31 and corrected 2026-11-01 under footplate-nqjf to what OpenAI's Live reference and four live runs of the spike harness settled (section 2 names the runs). A proposal as it was decided on, not a description of what the code does today; the decision is on the bead.

Design for bead footplate-vqze (flow `design.design`, stage `draft`). A yard is one installation of yardr: its beads, its crew and its depots. A crew member is an agent of the yard that a person talks with by mail; an ask is one question as data on a bead, which the person answers on its card, and a weighty ask passes the system's unlock, or the typing back of its number, before it is approved. footplate is the yard's native desktop window (Rust, gpui): a person reads conversations with the crew, users of the yard and people of linked yards, and answers asks there. A voice service is a provider outside the yard that hears speech and speaks back; in this design it is OpenAI's API. GPT-Live is OpenAI's full-duplex voice model (`gpt-live-1`): it listens and speaks at the same time and hands work that needs reasoning or tools to a backend, which OpenAI calls delegation. Client delegation is the form where the application itself is that backend. The Realtime API is OpenAI's other voice offer, where one model hears, reasons, calls tools and speaks inside a single session. See `docs/glossary.md` in the yardr repository for all of yardr's words.

## 1. The question

The bead, from mckean's idea of 31 Oct (mail 125856, 125861, as the bead quotes it): decide how a person talks with the yard by voice in footplate, where footplate holds a separate login or key for the voice service, what is said is handed to the yard as normal messages, and yardr does not change; to settle, as first asked, that the voice never invents yard facts, that while the yard works it says natural things like "let me check" or "let me think" and not "I'll ask", that asks are not approved by voice at first, that the feature is opt-in since what is read aloud goes to the provider, who holds the key, and the standing of the GPT-Live API (on which unofficial reports conflict, to be checked against OpenAI's own docs) against the documented Realtime models. This document does not call that hand-off a relay: Relay is the yard's connection product (mem-0dqf), and mckean asked that the two not be confused (note 17819). Three later mails narrow the question, and this design takes them as settled, not as questions: desktop only for now, phones out of scope, so the key is the person's own key on their own machine (mail 125883, note 17782); the design goes now, ahead of the launch-only rule (mem-rs6p), while building waits for the launch work (mail 125889, note 17783); and voice is off by default, enabled in footplate's settings with a key, which is also the opt-in (mail 125891, note 17784). The questions the first draft left open were then decided, by mckean and by the yardmaster as section 5 records; section 3 is drawn as decided, and two of those decisions change the first asking: the voice speaks the yard's answers in its own words, so a spoken answer may be wrong and the written mail is the record (section 3.4), and asks are unanswered by voice in the first version only, with the door left open to a later bead (section 3.5).

## 2. What exists today

**The conversation a voice would join is already there, and sending in it is one mail.** The window's chat mode (`Mode::Chat` in src/model.rs) holds conversations with a crew member or a user of this yard, or with one of a linked yard (`Target::Crew` and `Target::Peer` in src/model.rs; `Selected::Conversation` says which row is open). What a person types stands in a draft per conversation (src/draft.rs) in the one typed line (src/input.rs, gpui has no text field), and Enter sends it: `Footplate::send` in src/window/bead.rs takes the draft's text and attachments, refuses while the yard is reconnecting (nothing is queued, since a mail sent as the connection broke cannot be told apart from one that never arrived, and a queue could send twice), and calls `post` in src/window/rail.rs, which gives each file to the yard with `PutBlob` and then sends one `Mail`, or `MailPeer` to a linked yard, through `Client::mail` and `Client::mail_peer` in src/api.rs. Calls leave the window's thread through `Footplate::ask`, `ask_through` and `ask_in` in src/window/chrome.rs, which run the call on gpui's background executor with the yard's own client and hand the answer back to the window. The model itself calls nothing: `Effect` in src/model.rs is how it asks for its reads and acks, and the writes a person makes go by those window helpers direct. Nothing in the tree hears or speaks: voice and audio match nothing in src/, and the microphone is named in README.md only where the Mac bundle is said to do none of those things (it carries no entitlements, and the hardened runtime holds the microphone back), which bead 6 of section 6 must undo for the bundled app.

**Answers already arrive as events, with no polling.** A follower thread per yard (src/session.rs) logs in, reads the state whole and then follows the event stream (`Client::events` in src/api.rs), retrying with a backoff and replacing a stream that has said nothing for about two minutes; `FollowControl` stops it, waking a blocked read. The window applies each event through `Model::event` in src/model.rs, so a crew member's reply mail, a new ask and a change of crew state all reach the model as they happen, and the open conversation shows new mail where it stands. A voice that waits for the yard's answer needs no new way back: it waits on the same events.

**Asks are answered on their cards, and a weighty one passes a gate a voice must not pass for the person.** The card's calls are `Client::answer_ask`, `approve_ask`, `approve_setup`, `give_secret`, `defer_ask` and `decline_ask` in src/api.rs. How a card approves is `approval` in src/window/ask.rs: straight to the yard for an ordinary ask, through the system's unlock for a weighty one where one is offered (`available` and `evaluate` in src/unlock.rs, the Mac's LocalAuthentication and Linux's polkit behind them), after the ask's number is typed back where none is offered (`Approval::TypeBack`), and nowhere from this window where the yard takes no approval from it (`Approval::Nowhere`). Enter alone approves nothing on a run or setup card. There is today no path by which anything but the person's own key, tap and, for a weighty card, unlock or typed number reaches those calls.

**What footplate keeps, and where a secret may stand.** The settings are one JSON file in the platform's config directory (`Settings` in src/settings.rs, read and written whole on every change), with seven sections (`Section::ALL`: this window, appearance, notifications, yards, agents, connection, telemetry) drawn as a sheet over the window. A yard's device token and hosted session are never in that file: each stands in a file of its own beside it (`TOKEN_FILE` and the token and session files named per yard in src/yard.rs), read only where its mode is the owner's alone on unix and refused otherwise (`kept_as` in src/yard.rs), and written for its user alone. A secret typed in the window goes in a masked line (the same `Line` of src/input.rs drawn as a mark per character, which gives nothing out), as the Kratos password and a secret ask's value already do. README.md's "What it talks to" holds the window to the yard's calls (its "It reads" and "It writes" tables, which a test in src/api.rs keeps in step with the calls and with `READS`, the read calls a timeout may retry) and to two sites that are no yard's: yardr.dev for a fetched yardr, and PostHog's EU capture for the Feedback form. That form is the one place a person's words leave for a third party today: `src/feedback.rs` posts one event by `api::post_json` (a hand-written HTTP post in src/api.rs to a site that is no yard, carrying no yard token, session or device), with the project's write key as a source constant, and says in one plain line, held to the payload by a test (`PLAIN` in src/feedback.rs), all of what goes.

**The wire to a site that is no yard is hand-written HTTP over the system's TLS, and there is no socket that stays open to one.** `src/api.rs` is the whole client: HTTP/1.1 written by hand, no client library, a connection per call to the yard, and TLS by rustls against the system's roots (`Address::connect` and `tls()` in src/api.rs) for a yard at an address and for `post_json`, `read` and `download` to other sites. The one long-lived stream, the yard's events, is a GET answered a line at a time on the follower's thread. Cargo.toml has gpui pinned at 0.2.2 and no audio and no WebSocket crate: gpui draws and handles keys and the mouse, and offers no microphone, no speaker and no socket of its own. Tests run with no yard, no network and no screen, against stand-ins in src/fake.rs (a scripted `Yard` on a socket or on TCP, a `Router` for the edge, a `Posthog` for the capture), and the window on gpui's test platform.

**The two voice offers, as OpenAI's own docs stood on 31 Oct 2026.** I read them for this draft (the yardmaster's note 17781 read the same pages the same day and agrees). GPT-Live (`gpt-live-1`, https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-live-1): a full-duplex voice model whose one supported endpoint is `POST /v1/live/sessions`, spoken to over WebRTC or WebSocket; sessions cost $0.05 a minute billed per second, with any backend model's use billed apart, and rate limits counted in concurrent sessions. Its getting-started and delegation guides (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/live and https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/live-delegation) split the work exactly as this design does: the live model holds the spoken conversation, and delegated work goes to a backend the developer picks — a Responses model OpenAI runs, or, in client delegation, any agent or service of the application's own. In client delegation the session raises `session.delegation.created`, which carries a delegation id and a place on the session's timeline and, the guide says plainly, not the user's words: the application works out what was asked from the transcript events (`session.input_transcript.delta` for the person's speech, `session.output_transcript.delta` for the voice's) and its own state. The application returns what the backend found by `session.commentary.append`, which the model speaks, paraphrasing it, or keeps it unspoken in later replies by `session.thinking.append`. The two appends have the same four fields in the Live WebSocket reference (https://developers.openai.com/api/reference/resources/live/primary-websocket, read 1 Nov): `type`; `event_id`, the application's own identifier, optional there and sent on every append here so that an acknowledgement or an error can be matched to it; `delegation_id`, required and nullable, null for general session context and otherwise the id that `session.delegation.created` gave; and `content`, a plain string of at most 500 tokens. The field is `content`, not `text`. The reference schema marks it `minLength: 1`, while the Live delegation guide explicitly prescribes `content: ""` for the silent append that cancels an acknowledgement; this design follows the guide, and the build must verify that the endpoint accepts the empty value. The server acknowledges an append with `session.commentary.appended` or `session.thinking.appended`, each carrying `client_event_id`, the `event_id` of the append it answers, and an `error` event names a refused append in `error.client_event_id`. The acknowledgement says the content entered the session's timeline, not that it was spoken or played. Setting `delegation.acknowledgement_delay_ms` when the session is made (0 to 800, and 0 where it is not set; 800 in the guide's example) gives the application time to return the result before the model's automatic acknowledgement, and any thinking or commentary append cancels every pending one, those of other delegations too. The guide's recipe for keeping that acknowledgement from speaking is an empty `session.thinking.append` under the delegation's id, sent as soon as `session.delegation.created` arrives. All of it is a preference, not a promise of silence: the reference allows the model to speak before the delay, and an append does not stop audio already generated (3.5 and section 6, beads 1 and 2, for what the runs showed). Interrupting the voice leaves delegated work running, for the application to finish or cancel; a session's `store` stands at false unless the application asks for a stored recording to fork from; `session.started` carries the session's `expires_at` and the WebSocket reference gives `expired` as the close reason for the session duration limit, so a limit exists and each session is told its moment, though no page states the number it is set to. The prompting guide (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/live-prompting) gives the delegation policy as prompt text — when to hand work over, and "do not guess the result while waiting" — and puts permissions and confirmations on the application: the model asks, the application checks and runs. A later addition, native Decisions integration (changelog, 15 Oct; https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/decisions-voice), lets Live call the Decisions API directly to choose among actions the application defines and to decide whether the application should call Responses; it answers structured choices, not free-form spoken requests handed on as mail, so it does not replace client delegation here and is named only to keep this survey current. Over WebSocket (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/voice-websockets?api=live, which still recommends WebRTC) the application connects to `wss://api.openai.com/v1/live/sessions` with the project key as a bearer, sends `session.start` and waits for `session.started`, then streams mono PCM16 at 24 kHz, base64 in `session.input_audio.append`, and plays `session.output_audio.delta` in order. PCM on this socket is 16000 or 24000 Hz and no other rate (its other formats are G.711 at 8 kHz), one format serves both directions and cannot change during the session, and the application resamples what its devices give; 24 kHz is the guide's default and stands as this design's choice of the two. The audio deltas carry no timestamps and no event says the voice was interrupted, while the transcript deltas, input and output, carry start and end times and neither kind has a transcript-done event, so playback, and stopping it on interruption, is the application's own bookkeeping from what it has queued, and the end of an utterance is the application's own reading of a gap in the timeline (3.3, step 1). The key stands, in the guide's words, on a trusted server: no short-lived token for Live is documented, where the Realtime guide's browser flow has the server mint an ephemeral client secret. The Realtime API (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/realtime, model `gpt-realtime-2.1`) is the one-model shape: it hears, reasons with a configurable effort, calls the application's tools as function calls and speaks, in one session, and its guide carries a beta-to-GA migration (the beta header is gone, ephemeral secrets are made at `POST /v1/realtime/client_secrets`), which is the standing the bead asked to have checked for Live as well: the Live guides and the model's page state no such word, but OpenAI's API changelog for 10 Sep, before this design's date, says GPT-Live 1 is now generally available in the API (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/changelog), so the conflict the bead names, third-party pieces dating a general release against official silence, is settled by OpenAI's own record. What remains unstated is narrower: the numeric session limit above, and a short-lived form of the key. Voice agents (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/voice-agents) puts the choice itself: GPT-Live to keep an existing text workflow and choose its backend, Realtime where speech, reasoning and tool use should live in one session, a chained speech-to-text, agent and text-to-speech pipeline where the application wants to see and change the text between stages.

**What four live runs of the spike harness showed, 1 Nov 2026.** The harness in `spikes/voice-harness` (footplate-4hd3, then footplate-3rbp) is a throwaway program that holds a GPT-Live session over a hand-written WebSocket, plays what the model says, lets a person type the yard's answer and logs what they can read back; it is not product code. mckean ran it live on 1 Nov. The first two runs gave nothing to measure: run 1 was accepted and got no words either way, the silence was put down to a microphone fault on mckean's laptop, and run 2 has no log (notes 18265 to 18278). Runs 3 to 6 are the four that gave figures. mckean made them on Linux through ALSA's default device (48000 Hz, two channels, f32 samples), and they lasted 49, 39, 37 and 92 seconds, with one delegation in run 3, one in run 4, none in run 5 and two in run 6; each ended on `close_requested` with no error. Their logs are attached to notes 18282, 18284, 18285 and 18287 of footplate-nqjf, and each figure below that names a run is read from its log. They are one person's, on one machine, in sessions of a minute or two, and the harness's own thresholds (a -35 dBFS gate on the microphone, a -50 dBFS floor for what counts as the model's speech, a 300 ms holdoff) were guesses, not measurements; where a run shows one is wrong, the design says so. Four things have not been measured, and the design names each where it touches it: how captions and audio drift over minutes (mckean declined the five-minute run for now, note 18286), how long the voice takes to stop when the person speaks over it, echo on laptop speakers, and any live run on a Mac.

## 3. The proposal

The smallest voice that answers the question: one opt-in voice session at a time, bound to the conversation that is open, in which OpenAI's `gpt-live-1` in client-delegation mode holds the spoken side and footplate itself is the backend. A request that needs the yard is handed to it as the very mail Enter would send, the yard's answer comes back as the very mail it would send, and footplate gives that answer to the voice to speak in its own words, with the mail standing written in the conversation as the record (3.4). yardr does not change: no new call, no voice knowledge in the yard, and `yardr chat` in a terminal sees an ordinary conversation. What is new in footplate is a voice module, one settings section, one key file and the microphone and speaker paths the tree has never had; each is named as new where it stands below. The voice module is also the one place that speaks GPT-Live. Its events, its delegations and its commentary appends stay inside it, and the window and the yard hand-off speak to it in plain terms: speech started, a request, an answer to speak, stop. That is one seam and no plugin layer (mckean, mail 130257 of 1 Nov, taken as a decision by the yardmaster in note 18289): the design names no provider trait, no provider setting and no second implementation until a second backend exists, since one implementation cannot show the right shape for a second. mckean may want other backends later and has named ElevenLabs. Its API has not been checked here, and client delegation, which the hand-off below leans on, may be GPT-Live's alone, so a second backend may need more than a swap behind the same seam.

```
the person speaks ──► microphone ──► footplate ──wss──► GPT-Live (gpt-live-1)
                                          │  ▲              │ delegates:
                                          │  │              │ "this needs the yard"
                       the yard's mail ◄──┘  │              ▼
                       as it arrives         │     footplate works out the task
                                          │     from the transcript and sends
                       Client::mail ───────┘     it as one mail to the open
                       (no new yard call)        conversation; the answer comes
                                                 back on the event stream and
                                                 the voice speaks it in its
                                                 own words; the mail is the
                                                 written record
```

### 3.1 Turning it on, and the key

Voice is off until a person turns it on, which is the opt-in mckean settled (note 17784). A new section of the settings sheet, Voice, stands after Connection in `Section::ALL` (telemetry keeps its operator-only place at the end); the section is a switch, a masked key field, one plain line of what goes, and, while a session stands, its state in quiet words. The switch and the idle limit (section 5, decision 6) are settings like any other, kept in the settings file by `Settings::write`; the key itself never is. It stands in `voice.key` beside the settings file, written and read by the same keeping as a device token (`kept_as` in src/yard.rs, a marker line of its own so a token file is refused as one): on unix, a file others can read is refused with the reason, and what footplate writes is its owner's alone. Where `FOOTPLATE_OPENAI_KEY` is set in the environment it is read first and the file is not read, as `FOOTPLATE_TOKEN_FILE` already wins over the file beside the settings in src/yard.rs (section 5, decision 5). The key is OpenAI's and the variable says so; a second backend, if one comes, would bring a variable of its own named the same way, which costs nothing now, and nothing else about the key is made general for a backend that does not exist. The field is a masked `Line` (src/input.rs), as the Kratos password is: what is typed is in no draft, and Enter in it keeps the key and clears the field. The person's own OpenAI project key on their own machine is the trusted side the Live guide asks for (note 17782): footplate is that side, as it already is for the yard's device token. The plain line stands under the field in the Feedback form's manner, and a test holds it to what is sent, as `PLAIN` is held in src/feedback.rs:

> Microphone audio from this machine streams to OpenAI during a voice session you start, with what you say, the yard's name and the name of who you are talking with, and what they answer in the open conversation. The voice speaks answers in its own words, which may differ from the mail; the written mail in the conversation is the record. No saved session is kept with `store` off, but what was said may appear in OpenAI's abuse-monitoring logs, kept for up to 30 days by default. Your key stays on this machine, in a file beside the settings. Nothing is sent while voice is off, or while no session stands.

While the switch is off the open conversation shows no Voice button, the window opens no connection and reads no key: the switch itself always stands in the settings, and a person who never turns it on has the footplate of today.

### 3.2 The session: one, bound to the open conversation

A Voice button stands in the open conversation's head, beside its name, only where voice is on and a conversation row is open (a crew member or user of this yard, or one of a linked yard: the rows `post` already sends to, as `Target::Crew` and `Target::Peer`). It is a control, so it still clicks where a pane's top row stands under the Mac's title-bar strip (src/titlebar.rs), and no text field or drag of its own stands there with it. One tap starts the session, a second ends it; ending hangs up, sends `session.close` and waits for `session.closed`, as the WebSocket guide's ending does. The session belongs to that conversation and that yard's following: opening another conversation, switching yards, hiding or minimizing the window, or the window shutting ends it and stops the microphone, and a session for a held yard that is not open is never started. Its state is quiet words where the button stands — `listening`, `yardmaster is working` (the counterpart named as the row names them), `speaking` — in the design system's state words, with the session's minutes and what they have cost at Live's rate at the right, as counts stand quiet at the right; no badge, no pulsing mark.

Behind the button, a thread of footplate's own follows the voice session as src/session.rs follows a yard: it holds the WebSocket, the microphone stream and the playback queue it can drop on interruption, and stopping it wakes what it waits on, in the `FollowControl` manner, so a yard switch never waits for a socket. The server's audio stream does not pause between the model's words: it runs on at the pace of the clock, padded with silence, so audio arriving does not mean the voice is speaking, and the state word `speaking` cannot be read from its arrival. In run 3 the model's audio stood above -50 dBFS for only 6900 of 48280 ms, and in runs 4 to 6 for 6800 of 36520 ms, 9000 of 33520 ms and 17000 of 90080 ms; the first run received 32660 ms of audio in a session in which the model said nothing (note 18265). The queue ahead of the speaker stays short for the same reason: at each 15-second reading of runs 3 to 6 the speaker had played all but 0 to 60 ms of what had arrived. Captions and sound drift apart at no steady rate. The output caption's end minus the audio played grew from 200 to 400 ms at the first sample to 1110, 1095, 1490 and 1600 ms at the last (runs 3 to 6), at +1259, +1270, +2464 and +719 ms a minute over windows of 36, 32, 30 and 86 seconds. Those rates differ by more than three to one, and run 5's speech was cut twice, so the design promises no drift figure; over minutes it is unmeasured. It is the one new thread; nothing voice does runs on the window's thread, and its answers reach the window as the follower's `Update`s do. What that thread needs is new to the tree, and this design does not hide it: a WebSocket client over the rustls TLS `api.rs` already speaks, hand-written in the yard's way (an upgrade of the hand-written HTTP, then framed messages both ways: section 5, decision 3), and the microphone and speaker themselves, which give and take whatever format the device runs at (48000 Hz with two channels of f32 samples on the default ALSA device in all four runs), so the module converts to and from mono PCM16 at 24 kHz, one of the two PCM rates Live's socket takes (a new dependency in principle, the spikes to judge the crate against the systems' own calls behind `cfg`, as every other system call in the tree stands: section 5, decision 2). The session is made with `store` at its default false, the person's key as its bearer, the voice chosen in the settings or, where none is, the provider's default (section 5, decision 8), and no history seeded: it starts knowing the session's instructions (3.4), the open conversation's counterpart's name and the yard's name, both named in the plain line of 3.1 since both go to the provider, and nothing the yard has ever said. What it learns, it learns by appends during the session, and when the session ends footplate keeps none of it (the mails sent and received stand in the yard, as any mail does).

### 3.3 Handing it to the yard: delegation out as mail, the answer back as speech

The session's one backend capability, named in its instructions, is the open conversation itself. The round goes:

1. The person speaks. Their words reach the window as `session.input_transcript.delta` fragments, each with its start and end on the session's timeline; the window shows them as one caption line under the conversation's head, grown as they come, and keeps each finished utterance for the rule of step 2. The reference defines no turn and has no event that says an utterance is done, so footplate ends one where the timeline shows a gap. The harness starts a new caption at a gap of 700 ms, and that cut falls inside something that should stay whole twice in the logs: inside a noise tag in run 4 ('[sniff' ends at 5200 ms and '] What's blocking the release' begins at 6000 ms) and inside a question in run 6 ('Um. And what part is' ends at 38400 ms and 'outstanding there' begins at 40000 ms, a pause of 1.6 s). The length of the gap is a constant for the build to settle against those two, and the rule of step 2 reads the joined words and not one utterance at a time. The caption is not mail and is kept nowhere. It holds what no delegation has taken: a pause ends an utterance and not the caption (footplate-k3q7; the first draft had the next utterance replace it, and a spoken message that no delegation took was overwritten on screen and lost with it), so the line shows what will go, and it clears when a delegation takes the words (step 2), a request in them or none, and when the session ends. A delegation held behind a mail in flight, or raised while the person is still speaking (step 4), takes nothing, and the line keeps showing what will go when that mail lands and they have stopped. A caption longer than 120 characters, a line and a half of the usual window, shows its last words, from a whole word on, behind an ellipsis.
2. Where the request needs the yard, the model delegates (`session.delegation.created`). The event carries no words, so footplate takes the task from the transcript it holds. That rule, what text a delegation sends, is a plain function in the voice module, tested on its own, since what reaches the yard stands on sending what the person said and nothing the model composed. Of the person's finished utterances since the last delegation, the task is their requests, in the order they were said and joined, and the function makes three cuts. It keeps every request, and not the last only (footplate-k3q7 reverses the first draft's rule on purpose: the model raises no delegation for an utterance that needs none, its words stay held, and the last-only rule lost them, as mail 133790 reached porter as '. Does that make sense' with its first part gone; a stray extra phrase in a mail costs little, a lost sentence costs the message). Runs 3, 4 and 6 each sent the whole transcript since the last delegation as the task, so run 3 sent 'Hello  How's it going  What are we going to do  Um, well what's- what sort of work is happening'; what now goes is that whole minus its greetings, 'What are we going to do Um, well what's- what sort of work is happening'. It strips bracketed noise tags: the captions carry '[sniff', '[tongue click', '[sigh', '[laugh' and '[inhale', and one reached the task, in run 4 ('Hello  [sniff ] What's blocking the release'). A tag splits across transcript deltas, '[sniff' and then '] What's' in run 4 and '[sigh' and then '] [laugh' in run 6, so the tags go from the joined words and not fragment by fragment, and a ']' with no '[' before it at the start of the words is the end of a tag begun before them and goes with it. So does a '[' that nothing closes, but with its first word only (footplate-k3q7): the transcript also leaves a tag open with the person's words after it, as mail 133795 reached porter as '[sigh No, but my the one about the void platform, we can wait with that', where the tag's word is the tag and what follows is the person's. A tag's second word left open stays with the words, which costs little. A bracket that closes after more than 40 characters, or opens on a word longer than that, is the person's own and stays. And it leaves out greetings and chit-chat, ahead of the request and after it. A mark left at the start of the task, such as the '.' of mail 133790, is cut, and a mark that begins a later request ends the request before it, where the transcript sent it late. The function only cuts: whole words of the person's own, in the order they said them. It adds nothing, rewords nothing and joins nothing the model said. It is tested on the logs' transcripts, where the expected task is what the person asked: run 3's two questions, whole minus its greetings, run 4's 'What's blocking the release', and in run 6 the first question and the second, whole across its 1.6 s pause; and on the two mails above, and on two requests across a pause, which both go. Where the words hold no request at all, a greeting or thanks or a tag alone, it finds none, nothing is sent, and footplate closes the delegation in its own book. One case the same rule catches before any mail: where the final request asks only to hear the last answer again (the final request decides, as the first draft had it, so a request before it is not sent either, and an ask like it among other requests is for the voice and left out of the mail), nothing is sent to the yard; footplate gives that answer to the voice again as the `content` of a `session.commentary.append` under the new delegation's id, to be spoken again, and the delegation closes.
3. footplate sends that text as one mail to the open conversation, by `post` in src/window/rail.rs with an empty attachment list and the window's user as its sender, through `ask_through` so it lands in the yard it was spoken in whichever yard is open when it answers, where no earlier delegation waits (step 4 holds it otherwise). It sends under the same refusal as Enter: while the yard is reconnecting nothing goes, and the voice is told so (3.5 gives it the refusal's own words, once, under that delegation's id), the delegation closes, and the caption says the yard is away, rather than a queue forming that could send twice. The mail appears in the conversation as the person's own mail when its event returns, as a typed one does: the yard, the crew member and `yardr chat` see an ordinary message, with no mark that it was spoken.
4. footplate marks the delegation waiting on that conversation. A delegation waits from the moment its mail is sent, in the order they were sent, and closes when its answer is appended, when its refusal is given, or when the rule of step 2 finds nothing to send. The yard's answer is taken to be the next mail in it from the counterpart after the spoken mail's own event (for a crew member or user, their mail; on a linked yard's row, theirs through the peer) as `Model::event` applies it. That is a rule of thumb, not a correlation the yard gives: a mail carries who it is to, who it is from, its text and its files, and an event the same with its sequence, and neither names what it answers (`Client::mail` and `Event` in src/api.rs, `Letter` in src/model.rs), so an unasked mail from the counterpart during the wait would be taken for the answer; section 5, decision 10 settles that the first such mail is spoken as the answer for now. Counterpart mails are matched to waiting delegations oldest first, which, with one mail in flight (below), means the one that waits. A mail the person types in the open conversation while a session stands there (footplate-5j0f, amending decision 4 below) waits in the same order, with no delegation behind it: it is registered as sent when it goes, behind any spoken mail already in flight, and the first counterpart mail after it, matched oldest first exactly as a spoken mail's answer is, is taken for its answer — so the one-mail-in-flight matching and the unasked-mail risk of decision 10 are shared by typed and spoken mails alike. The answer's text, and only its text, goes back as the `content` of a `session.commentary.append` under the waiting delegation's own id and under no other, with a fresh `event_id` and never a null `delegation_id`, which is general session context that nothing ties to the question it answers. The one exception is a typed mail's answer: where the flight that waited holds no delegation because the person typed the mail, the append carries a null `delegation_id`, the one sanctioned null, since the person's own typed words in the conversation are what tie it to its question. A typed answer that lands while the person is speaking — their input transcript still arriving, by the end-of-utterance reading of step 1 — is held and appended only once they have stopped, so that speech never starts over them; nothing else is reordered. Past the 500-token limit it goes in chunks: split at sentence and then word boundaries by a plain function, each chunk the yard's text as appended, chunks appended under the one id, which the guide allows. Each append's `session.commentary.appended` is matched by its `client_event_id`, and an `error` that names the append is logged and leaves the mail as the record. The mail itself stands whole in the conversation for the person to read, and it, not the spoken paraphrase, is the record (3.4). A second counterpart mail is a second answer only if a second delegation waits for it; a mail that is the person's own (they typed meanwhile) never goes back. Two rules tie an answer to its delegation, and run 6 showed what happens without them. An answer is spoken only when a delegation waits for it, or where the mail it answers is one the person typed during the session: a counterpart mail that arrives with neither waiting is not appended for speech, whatever it says, and the mail in the conversation is its record. In run 6 the person typed 'everything' 220 ms before the second delegation opened; it went out with a null `delegation_id`, the server acknowledged it, and the model spoke it 442 ms after the delegation, as 'Everything for that zip.' ('for that zip' was the model's), in place of an acknowledgement. And a late answer is never spoken as a newer question's answer. That second delegation stayed open for 36 s, and its answer came in as the person asked a third question; the model raised no delegation for that question and held the answer until the person had finished speaking, about 2 s after it was appended, then spoke it, where it happened to fit. So a new finished utterance while a delegation waits is held as the next task (the rule of step 2 sends all the requests that are held, in the order they were said) and sent as mail only when that delegation closes: one mail is in flight, and the next counterpart mail can answer only it. The model raised no new delegation in that state in run 6, so footplate, not the model, keeps the order. When the delegation closes footplate sends the held task itself. If the model has raised a delegation for it by then, the mail is that delegation's and its answer is spoken under that id; if not, the mail goes out with none, and its answer finds no delegation waiting and is shown and not spoken, as above. The cost is plain: a correction spoken while the yard works reaches the yard after the first answer, where a typed one goes at once, and the first mail is not taken back, since a mail sent cannot be. Both rules rest on one session, run 6, with two delegations and one new question asked while one was open. A task is also taken only from a finished utterance (footplate-288j). While the person is still speaking, by the reading of step 1, a delegation the model raises is parked, as one is behind a mail in flight, and a mail that lands takes nothing. The words so far never go as a mail of their own with the rest going as another. The held task goes as one mail, whole, once the person has stopped and no mail is in flight, for the newest delegation parked. Words the model raised no delegation for stay held when the person stops. Nothing sends them on silence, and the model's next delegation takes them whole. The cost is a delay: a request goes to the yard when the gap of step 1 has passed since the person's last words, and not when the delegation arrives, if that is earlier.
5. Interruption does not rest on a level gate. The socket has no interrupted event and its audio no timing marker, and the voice stops by itself: in run 5, with the harness's own drop switched off, the server cut its speech as mckean spoke over it, twice in mid-sentence ('It lay' at 21000 ms and 'landed in a-' at 31200 ms), and the model then took up what mckean said. footplate adds two things, both keyed to the input transcript, the one signal the socket gives that the person is speaking. When the person's next utterance begins there (step 1), it drops the Live audio it has queued and not yet played, which is its own bookkeeping since the audio deltas carry no played marker, and for 300 ms after the drop it throws away any audio that arrives, the audio the server had already sent. The 300 ms is the harness's `holdoff` default, a figure chosen and not measured. The yard's work is left running, as the guide has it; its answer is still appended when it comes and spoken then (step 4), and the model, which heard the interruption, holds it against what the person says next. Runs 3 to 6 close the question of a fixed level gate on the microphone, which is how the harness decided that the person had begun to speak. At -35 dBFS the gate stood open for 49780 of 49820 ms in run 3, 37920 of 37960 ms in run 4 and 35200 of 35240 ms in run 5, and for 0 of 91780 ms in run 6, where mckean had changed the microphone's level (note 18288; its rms was -12.7, -12.7, -25.1 and -42.7 dBFS in the four runs). A gate that stays open sees no second onset, so the harness counted 0 interruptions in run 5 where there were two, and a gate that never opens sees none: no fixed gate opens and closes across ordinary settings, and neither barge-in nor the local drop rests on one. Not measured: how long the voice takes to stop once the person speaks, with the drop or without it. At the 15-second readings of runs 3 to 6 the speaker had played all but 0 to 60 ms of what had arrived, so little is queued to drop, and in both of run 5's interruptions the voice's last word ended on the timeline (at 21000 and 31200 ms) before the first input fragment of the interruption began (at 21400 and 32000 ms), a fragment that reaches the window 140 to 596 ms after its span ends (the harness's caption timeline against the clock, runs 3 to 6). Whether the drop and the holdoff remove anything the server has not already stopped is what a run with the drop on and a trigger that fires would show.

Nothing else reaches the voice. Notifications, other yards' mail, other conversations and the timetable are never appended: the session hears the open conversation's answers — to spoken requests under their delegations, and to mails the person typed there during the session under no delegation — and nothing else of the yard, and the person reads the rest where they read it today.

### 3.4 What "never invents" promises, as decided

The bead as first asked would have the voice never invent a yard fact at all. OpenAI's prompting guide is plain that the model chooses the wording for ordinary replies and that instructions steer it, so a model told never to guess can still speak a yard fact it was never given, and client delegation, the shape and the append rules constrain only what reaches the model, not what leaves its mouth. Holding the strict rule in speech would take a gate at the speaker that buffered the model's audio, judged each segment's transcript and played almost nothing in the model's own voice, with footplate reading the yard's mail aloud itself in a second speech voice. mckean decided against that (section 5, decision 9): the voice speaks the yard's answers in its own words, in one natural voice, and the design promises only what can be held — the yard's record is never invented. What that means, in the shape, the sends and the prompt, is this.

The record is the mails. The only words that reach the yard are the person's transcript, cut and never reworded by the tested rule of step 2 of 3.3, so the model cannot put a request of its own into the yard. What the voice is meant to speak from is the counterpart's mail as footplate appends it during the session, which stands written, whole, in the conversation beside the captions. The model is not bound to that source: it may answer directly without delegating, or state a yard fact it was never given in this session, in wording of its own — the delegation guide has what footplate appends spoken paraphrased, and the prompting guide has instructions steer the model rather than guarantee its speech. A spoken answer may therefore be wrong in more than paraphrase — an added, dropped or misstated fact, or one never given at all — and the plain line of 3.1 says so before anyone turns voice on, with the written mail as the record to read against it. There is no gate, no second speech path and no discarded speech to measure: model audio goes to the speaker as it comes, and an interruption drops what is queued when the person's next utterance begins (3.3, step 5).

The runs give the first figures on how faithful the paraphrase is, from three typed answers, and three is few. In run 4 the typed answer ('Everything else has landed. The Windows zip is still building, and three more things are waiting on it, so it will be a while yet.') came back with 100% of its content words kept and one reshape (', so it will be' became '. It'll be'); nothing was added. In run 6 two answers scored 75% and 83% on the harness's word count, and the count is of rewordings and not of lost content: 'Only the Windows zip is left.' was spoken as 'Only the Windows zip is still outstanding.', and the second answer's typo ('congestied') was corrected and its sentence split in two. No run caught the voice stating a yard fact it had not been given while it waited: run 3's 'Did you find anything' was answered 'Not yet, I'm still waiting.', and run 6's first wait, 16.6 s, was silent after 'Yeah, let me check.' Asked about itself in run 6, the voice said 'I watch what's in motion and bring you word from the yard when it's needed', claims about the product and not yard facts, which the product must make true (note 18287). The harness's score counts words and is a pointer to read the two lines by, not a verdict.

The shape and the prompt still steer what can be steered. Client delegation gives the model no tools and no backend but footplate, and footplate appends nothing as fact that did not arrive from the yard during the session: answers as the `content` of a `session.commentary.append` under their delegation's id, to be spoken, and, as the `content` of a `session.thinking.append`, unspoken, progress with no fact in it ("lookup in progress" is the guide's own example) and the ask direction of 3.5. Responses delegation, where OpenAI's model would be the backend reasoning over the yard, is not used. The session's instructions, a constant in the voice module held by a test to the words that carry the rule and to its three headings, say in the prompting guide's shape:

```
You are the voice of footplate, the window onto this yard. Speak plainly
and briefly. While you wait for the yard, say "let me check" or "let me
think"; never say you will ask anyone, and never name the machinery. If
a decision waits in the window, take no answer to it; tell the person
it waits there. The person is talking with {counterpart} in the yard;
everything that needs the yard is delegated, and footplate sends it as
their message and brings the answer back for you to speak. Speak each
answer briefly in your own words from what footplate gives you; the
written mail in the window is the record, so add nothing to an answer
that is not in what you were given.

Backchannel policy: Use no backchannels; listen until the person is done.

Interruption policy: Stop speaking when the user interrupts. Listen to what they say.

Delegation policy:
Backend tools:
- The open conversation: ask {counterpart}, and hear their answer.

Generate <|SpawnThinking|> when:
- The person asks anything of the yard, of {counterpart}, or that needs
  a fact you have not already been given in this session.
- The person tells {counterpart} something, or answers what
  {counterpart} asked, even if it asks nothing of the yard.
- A correction changes what was already asked.
- The person asks to hear an answer again; footplate gives it again.

Do not generate <|SpawnThinking|> when:
- The person greets you.
- You need a brief clarification before the request makes sense.

Generate <|SpawnThinking|> before giving an answer that depends on the
yard's work. Do not guess the result while waiting, and do not state a
yard fact you have not been given in this session.
```

The two policy headings are the prompting guide's, which asks that `Backchannel policy`, `Interruption policy` and `Delegation policy` stay, in that order, with the text beneath them ours. The lines under the Backchannel and Interruption headings are the ones the harness ran in runs 3 to 6. The voice is to speak only in answer or while it waits (3.5), so the Backchannel line allows no listening sounds over the person, and the Interruption line is the guide's template line. The guide says plainly that instructions encourage the model to yield but do not give precise control over when it stops speaking, and that it may keep speaking briefly after an interruption, which is why step 5 of 3.3 does not rest on them.

The bullet about telling and answering (footplate-k3q7) is there because a request goes to the yard only when the model raises a delegation, and the first lines had it raise one for what the person asks: words that tell {counterpart} something or answer what they asked stayed held with no mail. Whether the bullet makes the model delegate them is not known; it can only be heard in a live session.

`{counterpart}` is the open row's name as the list shows it. Where the model speaks a yard fact wrongly anyway, the written mail is untouched: the yard holds only the person's mail and its own answers, and the person who acts on a spoken name, number or state without reading it acts on spoken words the design has already warned may differ from the mail or have no mail behind them at all. That warning, and one natural voice with nothing buffered or discarded, is the price taken knowingly at `decide`; the strict gate remains describable from the first draft's history if a later bead wants it, at the delay and second voice it costs.

### 3.5 While the yard works, and the asks

While a delegation waits, the voice says the natural thing and not the machinery: never "I'll ask", never the backend, delegation or the yard's calls. `delegation.acknowledgement_delay_ms` stands at 800, the reference's maximum and the guide's figure, and the moment `session.delegation.created` arrives footplate sends an empty `session.thinking.append` under that delegation's id, the guide's recipe for keeping the automatic acknowledgement from speaking. The reason is that the voice should not promise a check before footplate knows whether one will be made: the mail may be held behind an earlier one or until the person has stopped, the words may hold no request, and the yard may be away (3.3).

The delay is what gives that append time to land, and the runs give the arithmetic, not a promise. Without the append the automatic acknowledgement spoke 1128 ms (run 3), 1154 ms (run 4) and 1110 ms (run 6, first delegation) after its delegation, after the 800 and not before it. The server acknowledged an append in 733 ms (run 4) and in 638, 674 and 684 ms (run 6), inside the delay by 67 to 162 ms, a margin a slow network can spend, and the guide says that acknowledgement confirms the context arrived, not silence. No run sent the empty append, so two things are unmeasured: whether it cancels the acknowledgement in time on a real wait, and what the voice says when it does. The 'Lemme check.' of runs 3 and 4 and the 'Yeah, let me check.' of run 6 were the automatic acknowledgement, which this append cancels. What stands in its place is the model's own wording under the instructions above ('let me check', 'let me think'), which no run has shown, and the window's state words carry the wait either way.

A commentary append, sent only under the waiting delegation's own id (3.3, step 4) — or under no delegation, where it is a typed mail's answer — cancels a pending acknowledgement too, as any append does, so an answer that comes before the model has spoken one takes its place. Run 6 has the one look at that, and it was an accident: the answer typed 220 ms before its delegation opened went out with a null id and was spoken in place of an acknowledgement, 442 ms after the delegation (the harness read that as an acknowledgement before the delay). That is one sample, and not a clean one, since it carried no delegation id. The typed answers of run 4 and of run 6's first delegation came after the acknowledgement had been spoken, so no run has watched an append under a delegation's id cancel an acknowledgement still pending.

Where the yard works longer (the counterpart's state in the model says working, which the crew list already reads), footplate sends no invented progress speech: one `session.thinking.append` of the progress kind, under the waiting delegation's id, when the wait passes half a minute, so a later answer still makes sense, and the window's state words say `yardmaster is working` where the person can see them. When the answer comes the voice speaks it (3.3 step 4); where the yard refuses the mail or the yard is away, the refusal's own words, which are the yard's, are what footplate gives the voice to speak, once, as the `content` of a `session.commentary.append` under that delegation's id.

Asks are not answered by voice in the first version, and that line is a first-version line, not a lasting rule: a later bead may add voice answers with safeguards of its own, and only a new decision would need to reopen this one if it were ever made lasting (section 5). The voice module holds no path to `AnswerAsk`, `ApproveAsk`, `ApproveSetup` or `GiveSecret`: it cannot name them, and the calls stay behind the cards' taps, keys and weighty gates exactly as today. Where an ask arrives in the voiced conversation while a session stands — the model knows, as it raises the card — footplate appends one direction by `session.thinking.append`, with a null `delegation_id` since no delegation owns a card, in words with no ask content beyond its standing, so the model takes no answer to it and tells the person in its own words that a decision waits in the window and needs a tap there, carrying no name and nothing of the question or its options. A person who answers by voice is told, in the session's instructions' words, to tap it in the window. A secret ask is not named even so far: nothing of it, not its bead nor its names, is appended and the voice says nothing either, since what is read aloud goes to the provider; the card waits in silence where it waits today. What the person says instead — a fresh request about the bead — is handed on as an ordinary mail, as `yardr chat` would carry it.

### 3.6 What the person sees, in one exchange

The settings' Voice section, as typed and seen: a switch at off; tapping it asks for the key in its masked field under the plain line of 3.1, and with a key kept the switch stands on and the section says `voice is on`. In the conversation with yardmaster, the head gains `Voice`; tapping it, the button says `listening` and the caption line stands empty. Then:

> Person (spoken, as the caption shows it): *What's blocking the release?*
>
> Voice: "Let me check." — and the caption gives the words to the mail that appears under them in the conversation, from the person: *What's blocking the release?*
>
> (yardmaster's row state reads working; the head says `yardmaster is working`.)
>
> Voice, on yardmaster's mail arriving, speaking it in its own words: "Everything else has landed — the Windows zip is still building." — words of its own over the mail in the conversation, which stands there as written and is the record if the two differ.
>
> Person, over the voice's last words: "Tell them to land without it if it's not done by four." The speaking stops, the words caption, delegate and go as a second mail; the first answer is not repeated.

In this exchange the voice's "Let me check." is the model's own wording, and whether it says it with the automatic acknowledgement cancelled is the unmeasured point of 3.5. The last line goes as soon as they have stopped speaking, because the first answer has arrived and its delegation has closed; spoken while the first delegation still waited, the same words would be held until it closed (3.3, step 4). Said in two remarks with a pause between them, and no delegation raised after the first, both stand in the caption and go together as the one mail that the next delegation makes.

Ending the session leaves the conversation exactly as if the mails had been typed: there is no voice record to keep, export or delete.

### 3.7 Endings and failures

A session that cannot be made — no key, a key OpenAI refuses, no network — says why beside the button in the yard's own manner ("OpenAI answered 401" is a refusal's words, shown, never parsed) and leaves the session unmade, never retrying unasked. The provider closing the session mid-conversation (its reference gives `expired` for the session duration limit and `session.started` carries `expires_at`, though the number is nowhere stated, so footplate reads the expiry when the session starts and expects it) ends it as a hang-up does: the caption clears, the button says `Voice` again, and nothing is re-seeded or resumed silently; a new tap starts a new session knowing nothing of what went before. The yard going away mid-wait is 3.3's refusal: the delegation closes, the voice says the yard does not answer, and the mail is not resent when the yard returns. A session left standing with nobody speaking closes itself after the idle limit (section 5, decision 6), since Live bills by the second; one a person ends closes at once. Logs (`src/log.rs`) take the session's openings, endings and refusals as they take the window's other sayings, without transcript or mail text.

## 4. Left out

**Phones, and Windows voice in the first build.** mckean settled desktop only (note 17782), which settles where the key stands and ends the question of where a phone would keep one; a phone is not this footplate in any case (README.md, Feedback). Windows footplate is a desktop window too, but its microphone consent, its audio path and a gate that runs build and test alone there (`.github/workflows/windows.yml`) make it a follow-on, not a first build (section 5, decision 7): the hand-off, settings and key beads are written so nothing in them is macOS or Linux but the audio calls behind `cfg`.

**Answering asks by voice in the first version.** No picking an option, no approving, no declining, no secret by speech in the first version — no more than that is ruled out: the bead asks it settled for the first version, mckean confirmed the line is for the first version only (section 5), and a later bead may add voice answers with safeguards of its own. The weighty gate (the unlock, the typed-back number) is a hand's proof that speech, which another person in the room could supply, should not stand in for without such safeguards (3.5). Reading an ask's question and options aloud is left out with it in the first version: what is read aloud goes to the provider, and an ask's context can carry what a mail would not.

**Responses delegation, native Decisions delegation and a Realtime build.** Responses delegation would make OpenAI's model the backend that reasons and answers, with footplate reviewing words already shaped by another model's reading of the yard: footplate would no longer be the backend handing work to the yard. Native Decisions delegation (section 2) answers defined choices and a whether-to-call-Responses question; it cannot carry a free-form spoken request on as mail. A Realtime session would reason and call tools itself, so footplate would have to give it the yard as function tools and trust its tool calls; each is more reasoning between the person and the yard than handing the message on, at more wiring. The chained pipeline (speech-to-text, a text model, text-to-speech) shows the text between stages, which this design does by sending mail, without a text model at all. All stay possible behind the voice module if Live disappoints (section 5, decision 1); none is proposed.

**A provider trait, a provider setting, and a second voice backend.** The voice module is the one seam (section 3), and nothing is built for a second backend before one exists: no trait that names what a backend does, no setting that picks one, no second implementation. mckean may want other backends and has named ElevenLabs (mail 130257, 1 Nov). The decision is one module with GPT-Live's wire detail inside it and plain terms outside, since a trait drawn from one implementation would show only that implementation's shape (note 18289). ElevenLabs' API has not been checked for this design. Client delegation, where the application is the backend and answers by appends under a delegation's id, may be GPT-Live's alone, and a backend without it would need the hand-off of 3.3 built another way, so a second backend may need more than a swap behind the seam. The key is the one thing named per provider, where that costs nothing (3.1).

**A gate at the speaker, a second speech voice, and a judge of the voice's words.** The strict holding of "never invents" — buffering the model's audio, judging each segment against a closed list and footplate reading the yard's mail aloud itself — is left out because mckean chose the natural voice over it (section 5, decision 9, section 3.4): its delay, its discarded clarifications and its second voice are not paid. A second model asked whether a paraphrase is faithful to the yard's text is left out with them, at a second model's cost and delay on every answer and with a judge that can itself be wrong. The safeguard that stands instead is the written mail beside the spoken words, already in the conversation; no check of spoken speech after the fact is kept, since the record needs none.

**Speaking anything but the open conversation's answers.** No notifications spoken, no other yard's or conversation's mail, no timetable, no bead notes by voice (`AddNote` stays typed on its card), no files by voice in either direction (attachments stay in the draft), and no session while the window is shut, hidden or minimized — hiding or minimizing the window ends a standing session and stops the microphone: the tray and menu-bar following goes on notifying as today, silently. Voice starts only by a tap in the open window and ends when that window is no longer showing.

**Keeping a voice record.** No transcript file kept by footplate, and `store` false at the provider, so no saved session recording is kept to download or fork from; microphone audio streams to OpenAI while the session stands, and what was said may appear in OpenAI's abuse-monitoring logs, retained for up to 30 days by default (the data-controls page for `/v1/live/sessions`, https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data), which the plain line of 3.1 names. Captions are gone with the session (3.2). The mails are the record, kept by the yard as any mail is; a person who wants the spoken words kept has them, as mail.

**A wake word and push-to-talk.** One session, started and ended by its button, in the voice chosen in the settings (section 5, decision 8). The model is full-duplex and hears while it speaks; a wake word is a listener standing open before the opt-in's session, which the off-by-default switch forbids in spirit; holding a key to talk fights the natural interruptions the bead asks for. Headsets against echo are recommended in the section's help line, not enforced. Echo on laptop speakers is unmeasured: runs 3 to 6 marked no input as the voice's own words (0 echo suspects in each summary), but no run was made to measure it (notes 18280 to 18287).

**Proactive speech.** The voice never starts speaking unasked: a mail that arrives with no delegation waiting (section 5, decision 4) and a crew member's state changing are shown, not spoken. The yard works for minutes at a time; a voice that reports on its own would talk over the person.

## 5. Open questions, now decided

The first draft left the ten questions below to the operator at `decide`, each with its options, its costs and the answer it recommended; the proposal was drawn as those answers would have it. They are all decided now, and this section records each decision with who took it, so the document can be read without the bead's notes. Nothing here is left open, and section 3 above is drawn as decided; a later change to any line below is a new decision, not a reading of this one. mckean decided questions 1 and 9 and the scope of the ask line; the yardmaster decided the rest under mem-gres, as the draft recommended, and mckean may overrule any of them (notes 17820, 17824, 17828).

1. **Build on GPT-Live. Decided by mckean (ask 17793).** GPT-Live in client delegation, as drawn in section 3: the only offer whose shape hands work to the application's own backend, with spoken and unspoken appends and the acknowledgement delay. It costs $0.05 a minute billed per second; in client mode there is no OpenAI-managed Responses backend charge, since the backend is footplate and the yard, while any costs the yard itself incurs in doing the work remain the yard's, separate from the session's bill. Its standing is settled in section 2: generally available since 10 Sep by OpenAI's changelog. What remains against it is narrower: its key has no short-lived form, so the desktop itself is the trusted side, and the numeric session limit is unstated (each session is told its own `expires_at`) — borne by a first build whose provider stands behind one module (section 6, bead 4), so leaving Live later is one module, not the window. The Realtime API and waiting were both set aside: Realtime would have footplate write and guard a tool surface and hold a reasoning model to its tool results, and waiting would only have slept behind the launch rule. The spikes' go or no-go (beads 1 and 2) is still read before the window is touched.
2. **A new audio dependency is acceptable in principle; the spikes judge it. Decided by the yardmaster (note 17820).** footplate has no audio path, and gpui offers none. A maintained cross-platform capture and playback crate may be taken where the spikes find one that passes — mono PCM16 at 24 kHz both ways on macOS and Linux, playback that can be dropped mid-queue, no C library the release scripts must learn to ship (the tree chooses pure-Rust where it can, and `scripts/release.sh` lists every archive member), and a licence the NOTICE file can carry — and the systems' own calls behind `cfg` (the `objc2` and CoreAudio family the tree already binds on the Mac, ALSA or PipeWire on Linux) stand where none does. The system's dictation reaches neither the continuous stream nor the droppable queue, so it is out either way. Found by the spike on 1 Nov, a finding and not a new decision: the harness took cpal 0.18.2 (Apache-2.0), which carried runs 3 to 6, and its Linux backend is ALSA's, so it needs libasound (`libasound2-dev` to build, `libasound.so.2` to run), the one C library the criterion above wanted none of. The shipping bead's archive and the Debian build container (`scripts/linux-container.sh`, whose package list does not have it) must carry it (section 6, bead 6). The devices ran at 48000 Hz with two channels of f32 samples, so "mono PCM16 at 24 kHz both ways" is the module's conversion (3.2), and the socket takes PCM at 16000 or 24000 Hz and no other rate (section 2).
3. **The WebSocket is hand-written. Decided by the yardmaster (note 17820).** Hand-written in the voice module over `api.rs`'s TLS: an HTTP upgrade, then client-masked frames, fragmentation and ping and pong, some hundreds of lines with their tests, in the tree's stated way ("HTTP hand-written, no client library", AGENTS.md) and adding nothing to Cargo.toml. Its risk is a framing fault only a real session shakes out, which the spikes are for; a crate would have been quicker to a speaking session at one more dependency. One correction to "adding nothing to Cargo.toml": the upgrade's answer carries `Sec-WebSocket-Accept`, the base64 of the SHA-1 of the client's key and a fixed string, and the client checks it, so the module needs SHA-1 once, with no security in it. The tree has `sha2` and no SHA-1, but `sha1_smol` is already in Cargo.lock, as `uuid`'s dependency: naming it in Cargo.toml adds one line there and no new package to Cargo.lock. The harness wrote its own instead, some forty lines tested against FIPS 180-4's vectors and RFC 6455's example key (`spikes/voice-harness/src/ws.rs`), which is the other way. Either keeps the tree's way, with no WebSocket crate.
4. **Only awaited answers are spoken. Decided by the yardmaster (note 17820); the typed half amended by mckean on yd-5hwm (ask 18569, footplate-5j0f).** A crew member may write unasked while a session stands; such mail is shown in the conversation, and the head says new mail stands there, but it is not spoken. Speaking every counterpart mail was set aside (a voice that talks over the person, at per-minute cost), as was speaking only the first line (an interruption with a cut-off rule of its own). Run 6 showed the rule's edge: an answer typed when no delegation waited went out with no id and was spoken, which step 4 of 3.3 now forbids; a mail with no delegation waiting is shown and not spoken. Amended: an answer to a mail the person themselves typed in the open conversation during the session is awaited too, and is spoken under no delegation (the one sanctioned null of step 4), once the person has stopped talking; mckean picked this on yd-5hwm, accepting the cost in voice minutes. A typed mail in another conversation, or in another yard, awaits nothing there.
5. **The key is typed in the settings, with the environment read first. Decided by the yardmaster (note 17820).** Typed in the Voice section's masked field and kept in `voice.key` beside the settings (3.1): a person with no terminal can do it, which is footplate's purpose. `FOOTPLATE_OPENAI_KEY`, where set, is read first and the file is not read. A file-only section (nothing secret ever typed into the window, at the cost of asking a terminal of exactly the person footplate exists to spare one) was set aside.
6. **A session closes itself after five idle minutes. Decided by the yardmaster (note 17820).** After five minutes with nobody speaking and no delegation waiting, and the head counts its minutes and cost meanwhile (3.2), so a forgotten session bills at most 25 cents past its last use; a working conversation, which speaks or waits, never meets the limit. The five is a setting beside the switch. No idle limit (a forgotten session billing until the provider ends it) and a hard length as well (a session cut mid-work, restarted cold) were both set aside. Live's numeric duration limit stays undocumented; each session is told its own `expires_at` (3.7).
7. **macOS and Linux first; Windows after. Decided by the yardmaster (note 17820).** The audio spikes prove two systems, the Mac's microphone consent (`NSMicrophoneUsageDescription`, which `scripts/bundle-mac.sh` must carry) and a Linux desktop's are known quantities, and the Windows job builds and tests but cannot listen. On 1 Nov only Linux had run live, and no run on a Mac had shown words (section 2). The settings, key and hand-off beads are system-free, so Windows follows by its audio path alone. All three together (a third audio proof and a consent story nobody could hear before it shipped, on the only platform with no yard of its own) was set aside. Amended: the microphone a session hears through is the system's default until a person picks another in the Voice settings (mckean, by voice, filed by porter: footplate-aqgc). The choice is the microphone's id as the host gives it (a Mac's UID), kept in the settings file only while one is picked; the list is the machine's own, read when the section shows and when the list opens, and opens no device, since ALSA opens one to read its format. The devices open as a session starts, so a pick is used from the next session and never moves one that stands; a picked microphone the host no longer lists (unplugged, or named otherwise) falls back to the default, with the session naming the microphone that stands, and the field says it was not found. The speaker stays the default's, one microphone serves every conversation, and a mid-session switch is left out.
8. **The provider's default voice. Decided by the yardmaster (note 17820); the voice setting added by mckean on 2 Nov (mail 133630, footplate-t7jr).** Live makes the voice (`audio.output.voice`, `marin` in the guides' examples) and model at the session's start and changes neither standing; the first version takes the default and keeps no voice setting, so every person hears the same voice while the feature is judged and the new section holds one choice fewer. OpenAI's changelog of 16 Oct added nine language-specific natural voices with prompt-to-voice creation, and the first version still takes the provider's default. A short list of documented voices beside the switch (a sitting to record the list, a session restarted to change it) was set aside. Language follows the person's speech either way. Amended: mckean asked for the choice (mail 133630), and the Voice section has a picker of the voices OpenAI's Live reference lists, 31 built-in names, each with the guide's note of language and accent where the guide has one, and `default` first, which sends no voice and keeps nothing in the settings file. The voice is `audio.output.voice` of `session.start`, so a pick is heard from the next session and never changes one that stands, as the reference has it ("cannot change after startup"), and the picker says so. A custom voice is an account's own and is not offered; a name the settings file holds that OpenAI refuses is refused in the session's own refusal words. Per-crew voices and a preview of a voice are left to later beads.
9. **Natural: the voice speaks answers in its own words. Decided by mckean (ask 17821, note 17824).** No gate at the speaker, no second speech path: the model's paraphrase of an answer is played as it comes, and the design promises only that the yard's record is never invented — the person's mail is their own words and the mail in the window is the yard's — while accepting that a spoken paraphrase may state a yard fact wrongly or one never given, corrected only by the written mail beside it (3.4). The strict holding (buffered audio judged segment by segment, only a closed list played in the model's voice, yard answers read word for word by footplate's own speech path, all else discarded unheard) was set aside with its delay, its discarded clarifications and its second voice; section 4 keeps it named, and the spikes no longer measure discards, since there are none.
10. **The first counterpart mail during a wait is taken for the answer. Decided by the yardmaster (note 17820).** Section 3.3, step 4 matches by order because no mail or event names what it answers: the first mail from the counterpart after the spoken mail, matched to the oldest waiting delegation, is appended and spoken as its answer. It is wrong whenever the counterpart writes unasked during the wait, or answers the second of two spoken mails first; a spoken correction follows a misreading as a typed one does, and the mail stands written. Never assuming (every waiting mail shown and spoken only when asked) and assuming only while one delegation waits were both set aside for now; if misreadings prove common, assuming only while one waits is the agreed next step, without new machinery. Since 1 Nov step 4 of 3.3 holds a second request until the first delegation closes, so one spoken mail is in flight and the second of two mails being answered first no longer arises from footplate's side; the unasked mail during a wait remains. A typed mail during a session shares this risk alike (footplate-5j0f): it takes its place behind any mail already in flight, the first counterpart mail after it is taken for its answer, and an unasked mail in that wait would be spoken as the typed mail's answer, as it would be for a spoken one.

Beyond the ten: **asks are unanswered by voice in the first version only. Decided by mckean (ask 17825).** The first version behaves as 3.5 draws either way; what this settles is the wording — the door is left open, and a later bead may add voice answers with safeguards of its own. Making the line lasting (cards always answered on screen, reopenable only by a new decision) was set aside.
