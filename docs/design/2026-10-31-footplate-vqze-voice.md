# Talk with the yard by voice in footplate

Design footplate-vqze, proposed 2026-10-31. A proposal as it was decided on, not a description of what the code does today; the decision is on the bead.

Design for bead footplate-vqze (flow `design.design`, stage `draft`). A yard is one installation of yardr: its beads, its crew and its depots. A crew member is an agent of the yard that a person talks with by mail; an ask is one question as data on a bead, which the person answers on its card, and a weighty ask passes the system's unlock, or the typing back of its number, before it is approved. footplate is the yard's native desktop window (Rust, gpui): a person reads conversations with the crew, users of the yard and people of linked yards, and answers asks there. A voice service is a provider outside the yard that hears speech and speaks back; in this design it is OpenAI's API. GPT-Live is OpenAI's full-duplex voice model (`gpt-live-1`): it listens and speaks at the same time and hands work that needs reasoning or tools to a backend, which OpenAI calls delegation. Client delegation is the form where the application itself is that backend. The Realtime API is OpenAI's other voice offer, where one model hears, reasons, calls tools and speaks inside a single session. See `docs/glossary.md` in the yardr repository for all of yardr's words.

## 1. The question

The bead, from mckean's idea of 31 Oct (mail 125856, 125861, as the bead quotes it): decide how a person talks with the yard by voice in footplate, where footplate holds a separate login or key for the voice service, what is said is handed to the yard as normal messages, and yardr does not change; to settle, as first asked, that the voice never invents yard facts, that while the yard works it says natural things like "let me check" or "let me think" and not "I'll ask", that asks are not approved by voice at first, that the feature is opt-in since what is read aloud goes to the provider, who holds the key, and the standing of the GPT-Live API (on which unofficial reports conflict, to be checked against OpenAI's own docs) against the documented Realtime models. This document does not call that hand-off a relay: Relay is the yard's connection product (mem-0dqf), and mckean asked that the two not be confused (note 17819). Three later mails narrow the question, and this design takes them as settled, not as questions: desktop only for now, phones out of scope, so the key is the person's own key on their own machine (mail 125883, note 17782); the design goes now, ahead of the launch-only rule (mem-rs6p), while building waits for the launch work (mail 125889, note 17783); and voice is off by default, enabled in footplate's settings with a key, which is also the opt-in (mail 125891, note 17784). The questions the first draft left open were then decided, by mckean and by the yardmaster as section 5 records; section 3 is drawn as decided, and two of those decisions change the first asking: the voice speaks the yard's answers in its own words, so a spoken answer may be wrong and the written mail is the record (section 3.4), and asks are unanswered by voice in the first version only, with the door left open to a later bead (section 3.5).

## 2. What exists today

**The conversation a voice would join is already there, and sending in it is one mail.** The window's chat mode (`Mode::Chat` in src/model.rs) holds conversations with a crew member or a user of this yard, or with one of a linked yard (`Target::Crew` and `Target::Peer` in src/model.rs; `Selected::Conversation` says which row is open). What a person types stands in a draft per conversation (src/draft.rs) in the one typed line (src/input.rs, gpui has no text field), and Enter sends it: `Footplate::send` in src/window/bead.rs takes the draft's text and attachments, refuses while the yard is reconnecting (nothing is queued, since a mail sent as the connection broke cannot be told apart from one that never arrived, and a queue could send twice), and calls `post` in src/window/rail.rs, which gives each file to the yard with `PutBlob` and then sends one `Mail`, or `MailPeer` to a linked yard, through `Client::mail` and `Client::mail_peer` in src/api.rs. Calls leave the window's thread through `Footplate::ask`, `ask_through` and `ask_in` in src/window/chrome.rs, which run the call on gpui's background executor with the yard's own client and hand the answer back to the window. The model itself calls nothing: `Effect` in src/model.rs is how it asks for its reads and acks, and the writes a person makes go by those window helpers direct. Nothing in the tree hears or speaks: voice and audio match nothing in src/, and the microphone is named in README.md only where the Mac bundle is said to do none of those things (it carries no entitlements, and the hardened runtime holds the microphone back), which bead 6 of section 6 must undo for the bundled app.

**Answers already arrive as events, with no polling.** A follower thread per yard (src/session.rs) logs in, reads the state whole and then follows the event stream (`Client::events` in src/api.rs), retrying with a backoff and replacing a stream that has said nothing for about two minutes; `FollowControl` stops it, waking a blocked read. The window applies each event through `Model::event` in src/model.rs, so a crew member's reply mail, a new ask and a change of crew state all reach the model as they happen, and the open conversation shows new mail where it stands. A voice that waits for the yard's answer needs no new way back: it waits on the same events.

**Asks are answered on their cards, and a weighty one passes a gate a voice must not pass for the person.** The card's calls are `Client::answer_ask`, `approve_ask`, `approve_setup`, `give_secret`, `defer_ask` and `decline_ask` in src/api.rs. How a card approves is `approval` in src/window/ask.rs: straight to the yard for an ordinary ask, through the system's unlock for a weighty one where one is offered (`available` and `evaluate` in src/unlock.rs, the Mac's LocalAuthentication and Linux's polkit behind them), after the ask's number is typed back where none is offered (`Approval::TypeBack`), and nowhere from this window where the yard takes no approval from it (`Approval::Nowhere`). Enter alone approves nothing on a run or setup card. There is today no path by which anything but the person's own key, tap and, for a weighty card, unlock or typed number reaches those calls.

**What footplate keeps, and where a secret may stand.** The settings are one JSON file in the platform's config directory (`Settings` in src/settings.rs, read and written whole on every change), with seven sections (`Section::ALL`: this window, appearance, notifications, yards, agents, connection, telemetry) drawn as a sheet over the window. A yard's device token and hosted session are never in that file: each stands in a file of its own beside it (`TOKEN_FILE` and the token and session files named per yard in src/yard.rs), read only where its mode is the owner's alone on unix and refused otherwise (`kept_as` in src/yard.rs), and written for its user alone. A secret typed in the window goes in a masked line (the same `Line` of src/input.rs drawn as a mark per character, which gives nothing out), as the Kratos password and a secret ask's value already do. README.md's "What it talks to" holds the window to the yard's calls (its "It reads" and "It writes" tables, which a test in src/api.rs keeps in step with the calls and with `READS`, the read calls a timeout may retry) and to two sites that are no yard's: yardr.dev for a fetched yardr, and PostHog's EU capture for the Feedback form. That form is the one place a person's words leave for a third party today: `src/feedback.rs` posts one event by `api::post_json` (a hand-written HTTP post in src/api.rs to a site that is no yard, carrying no yard token, session or device), with the project's write key as a source constant, and says in one plain line, held to the payload by a test (`PLAIN` in src/feedback.rs), all of what goes.

**The wire to a site that is no yard is hand-written HTTP over the system's TLS, and there is no socket that stays open to one.** `src/api.rs` is the whole client: HTTP/1.1 written by hand, no client library, a connection per call to the yard, and TLS by rustls against the system's roots (`Address::connect` and `tls()` in src/api.rs) for a yard at an address and for `post_json`, `read` and `download` to other sites. The one long-lived stream, the yard's events, is a GET answered a line at a time on the follower's thread. Cargo.toml has gpui pinned at 0.2.2 and no audio and no WebSocket crate: gpui draws and handles keys and the mouse, and offers no microphone, no speaker and no socket of its own. Tests run with no yard, no network and no screen, against stand-ins in src/fake.rs (a scripted `Yard` on a socket or on TCP, a `Router` for the edge, a `Posthog` for the capture), and the window on gpui's test platform.

**The two voice offers, as OpenAI's own docs stood on 31 Oct 2026.** I read them for this draft (the yardmaster's note 17781 read the same pages the same day and agrees). GPT-Live (`gpt-live-1`, https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-live-1): a full-duplex voice model whose one supported endpoint is `POST /v1/live/sessions`, spoken to over WebRTC or WebSocket; sessions cost $0.05 a minute billed per second, with any backend model's use billed apart, and rate limits counted in concurrent sessions. Its getting-started and delegation guides (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/live and https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/live-delegation) split the work exactly as this design does: the live model holds the spoken conversation, and delegated work goes to a backend the developer picks — a Responses model OpenAI runs, or, in client delegation, any agent or service of the application's own. In client delegation the session raises `session.delegation.created`, which carries a delegation id and a place on the session's timeline and, the guide says plainly, not the user's words: the application works out what was asked from the transcript events (`session.input_transcript.delta` for the person's speech, `session.output_transcript.delta` for the voice's) and its own state. The application returns what the backend found by `session.commentary.append`, which the model speaks, paraphrasing it, or keeps it unspoken in later replies by `session.thinking.append`, each append a plain string of at most 500 tokens, matched to its delegation by id; setting `delegation.acknowledgement_delay_ms` when the session is made (800 in the guide's example) gives the application time to return the result before the model's automatic acknowledgement, and a thinking or commentary append cancels every pending one — a preference, not a promise of silence, since the session reference allows the model to speak before the delay and 800 is its maximum, so the spikes must watch for an early acknowledgement and for its cancellation (section 6, beads 1 and 2). Interrupting the voice leaves delegated work running, for the application to finish or cancel; a session's `store` stands at false unless the application asks for a stored recording to fork from; `session.started` carries the session's `expires_at` and the WebSocket reference gives `expired` as the close reason for the session duration limit, so a limit exists and each session is told its moment, though no page states the number it is set to. The prompting guide (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/live-prompting) gives the delegation policy as prompt text — when to hand work over, and "do not guess the result while waiting" — and puts permissions and confirmations on the application: the model asks, the application checks and runs. A later addition, native Decisions integration (changelog, 15 Oct; https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/decisions-voice), lets Live call the Decisions API directly to choose among actions the application defines and to decide whether the application should call Responses; it answers structured choices, not free-form spoken requests handed on as mail, so it does not replace client delegation here and is named only to keep this survey current. Over WebSocket (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/voice-websockets?api=live, which still recommends WebRTC) the application connects to `wss://api.openai.com/v1/live/sessions` with the project key as a bearer, sends `session.start` and waits for `session.started`, then streams mono PCM16 at 24 kHz, base64 in `session.input_audio.append`, and plays `session.output_audio.delta` in order; the audio deltas carry no timestamps, while the output-transcript deltas carry start and end times and have no transcript-done event, so playback, and stopping it on interruption, is the application's own bookkeeping from what it has queued. The key stands, in the guide's words, on a trusted server: no short-lived token for Live is documented, where the Realtime guide's browser flow has the server mint an ephemeral client secret. The Realtime API (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/realtime, model `gpt-realtime-2.1`) is the one-model shape: it hears, reasons with a configurable effort, calls the application's tools as function calls and speaks, in one session, and its guide carries a beta-to-GA migration (the beta header is gone, ephemeral secrets are made at `POST /v1/realtime/client_secrets`), which is the standing the bead asked to have checked for Live as well: the Live guides and the model's page state no such word, but OpenAI's API changelog for 10 Sep, before this design's date, says GPT-Live 1 is now generally available in the API (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/changelog), so the conflict the bead names, third-party pieces dating a general release against official silence, is settled by OpenAI's own record. What remains unstated is narrower: the numeric session limit above, and a short-lived form of the key. Voice agents (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/voice-agents) puts the choice itself: GPT-Live to keep an existing text workflow and choose its backend, Realtime where speech, reasoning and tool use should live in one session, a chained speech-to-text, agent and text-to-speech pipeline where the application wants to see and change the text between stages.

## 3. The proposal

The smallest voice that answers the question: one opt-in voice session at a time, bound to the conversation that is open, in which OpenAI's `gpt-live-1` in client-delegation mode holds the spoken side and footplate itself is the backend. What the person asks for is sent to the yard as the very mail Enter would send, the yard's answer comes back as the very mail it would send, and footplate gives that answer to the voice to speak in its own words, with the mail standing written in the conversation as the record (3.4). yardr does not change: no new call, no voice knowledge in the yard, and `yardr chat` in a terminal sees an ordinary conversation. What is new in footplate is a voice module, one settings section, one key file and the microphone and speaker paths the tree has never had; each is named as new where it stands below.

```
the person speaks ──► microphone ──► footplate ──wss──► GPT-Live (gpt-live-1)
                                          │  ▲              │ delegates:
                                          │  │              │ "this needs the yard"
                       the yard's mail ◄──┘  │              ▼
                       as it arrives         │     footplate works out the task
                                          │     from the transcript and sends
                       Client::mail ───────┘     it as one mail to the open
                       (no new yard call)        conversation; the answer comes
                                                 back on the event stream and
                                                 the voice speaks it in its
                                                 own words; the mail is the
                                                 written record
```

### 3.1 Turning it on, and the key

Voice is off until a person turns it on, which is the opt-in mckean settled (note 17784). A new section of the settings sheet, Voice, stands after Connection in `Section::ALL` (telemetry keeps its operator-only place at the end); the section is a switch, a masked key field, one plain line of what goes, and, while a session stands, its state in quiet words. The switch and the idle limit (section 5, decision 6) are settings like any other, kept in the settings file by `Settings::write`; the key itself never is. It stands in `voice.key` beside the settings file, written and read by the same keeping as a device token (`kept_as` in src/yard.rs, a marker line of its own so a token file is refused as one): on unix, a file others can read is refused with the reason, and what footplate writes is its owner's alone. Where `FOOTPLATE_OPENAI_KEY` is set in the environment it is read first and the file is not read, as `FOOTPLATE_TOKEN_FILE` already wins over the file beside the settings in src/yard.rs (section 5, decision 5). The field is a masked `Line` (src/input.rs), as the Kratos password is: what is typed is in no draft, and Enter in it keeps the key and clears the field. The person's own OpenAI project key on their own machine is the trusted side the Live guide asks for (note 17782): footplate is that side, as it already is for the yard's device token. The plain line stands under the field in the Feedback form's manner, and a test holds it to what is sent, as `PLAIN` is held in src/feedback.rs:

> Microphone audio from this machine streams to OpenAI while voice is on, with what you say, the yard's name and the name of who you are talking with, and what they answer in the open conversation. The voice speaks answers in its own words, which may differ from the mail; the written mail in the conversation is the record. No saved session is kept with `store` off, but what was said may appear in OpenAI's abuse-monitoring logs, kept for up to 30 days by default. Your key stays on this machine, in a file beside the settings. Nothing is sent while voice is off.

While the switch is off the window shows no voice control anywhere, opens no connection and reads no key: a person who never turns it on has the footplate of today.

### 3.2 The session: one, bound to the open conversation

A Voice button stands in the open conversation's head, beside its name, only where voice is on and a conversation row is open (a crew member or user of this yard, or one of a linked yard: the rows `post` already sends to, as `Target::Crew` and `Target::Peer`). It is a control, so it still clicks where a pane's top row stands under the Mac's title-bar strip (src/titlebar.rs), and no text field or drag of its own stands there with it. One tap starts the session, a second ends it; ending hangs up, sends `session.close` and waits for `session.closed`, as the WebSocket guide's ending does. The session belongs to that conversation and that yard's following: opening another conversation, switching yards, hiding or minimizing the window, or the window shutting ends it and stops the microphone, and a session for a held yard that is not open is never started. Its state is quiet words where the button stands — `listening`, `yardmaster is working` (the counterpart named as the row names them), `speaking` — in the design system's state words, with the session's minutes and what they have cost at Live's rate at the right, as counts stand quiet at the right; no badge, no pulsing mark.

Behind the button, a thread of footplate's own follows the voice session as src/session.rs follows a yard: it holds the WebSocket, the microphone stream and the playback queue it can drop on interruption, and stopping it wakes what it waits on, in the `FollowControl` manner, so a yard switch never waits for a socket. It is the one new thread; nothing voice does runs on the window's thread, and its answers reach the window as the follower's `Update`s do. What that thread needs is new to the tree, and this design does not hide it: a WebSocket client over the rustls TLS `api.rs` already speaks, hand-written in the yard's way (an upgrade of the hand-written HTTP, then framed messages both ways: section 5, decision 3), and the microphone and speaker themselves, mono PCM16 at 24 kHz as Live's socket takes (a new dependency in principle, the spikes to judge the crate against the systems' own calls behind `cfg`, as every other system call in the tree stands: section 5, decision 2). The session is made with `store` at its default false, the person's key as its bearer, the provider's default voice with no voice setting (section 5, decision 8), and no history seeded: it starts knowing the session's instructions (3.4), the open conversation's counterpart's name and the yard's name, both named in the plain line of 3.1 since both go to the provider, and nothing the yard has ever said. What it learns, it learns by appends during the session, and when the session ends footplate keeps none of it (the mails sent and received stand in the yard, as any mail does).

### 3.3 Handing it to the yard: delegation out as mail, the answer back as speech

The session's one backend capability, named in its instructions, is the open conversation itself. The round goes:

1. The person speaks. Their words reach the window as `session.input_transcript.delta` fragments; the window shows them as one caption line under the conversation's head, replaced as they grow, and keeps the finished utterance as the standing task text. The caption is not mail, is kept nowhere, and goes when the session ends or the next utterance begins.
2. Where the request needs the yard, the model delegates (`session.delegation.created`). The event carries no words, so footplate takes the task from the transcript it holds: the person's finished utterances since the last delegation, joined as typed. That rule — what text a delegation sends — is a plain function in the voice module, tested on its own, since what reaches the yard stands on sending what the person said and nothing the model composed. One case the same rule catches before any mail: where the finished utterances ask only to hear the last answer again, nothing is sent to the yard; footplate gives that answer to the voice again by `session.commentary.append` under the new delegation's id, to be spoken again, and the delegation closes.
3. footplate sends that text as one mail to the open conversation, by `post` in src/window/rail.rs with an empty attachment list and the window's user as its sender, through `ask_through` so it lands in the yard it was spoken in whichever yard is open when it answers. It sends under the same refusal as Enter: while the yard is reconnecting nothing goes, and the voice is told so (the delegation closes unspoken, and the caption says the yard is away) rather than a queue forming that could send twice. The mail appears in the conversation as the person's own mail when its event returns, as a typed one does: the yard, the crew member and `yardr chat` see an ordinary message, with no mark that it was spoken.
4. footplate marks the delegation waiting on that conversation, in the order delegations were sent. The yard's answer is taken to be the next mail in it from the counterpart after the spoken mail's own event (for a crew member or user, their mail; on a linked yard's row, theirs through the peer) as `Model::event` applies it. That is a rule of thumb, not a correlation the yard gives: a mail carries who it is to, who it is from, its text and its files, and an event the same with its sequence, and neither names what it answers (`Client::mail` and `Event` in src/api.rs, `Letter` in src/model.rs), so an unasked mail from the counterpart during the wait would be taken for the answer; section 5, decision 10 settles that the first such mail is spoken as the answer for now. Where two delegations wait, counterpart mails are matched to them oldest first. The answer's text, and only its text, goes back by `session.commentary.append` under the waiting delegation's id, for the voice to speak in its own words, in chunks where it passes the 500-token append limit: split at sentence and then word boundaries by a plain function, each chunk the yard's text as appended, chunks appended under the one id, which the guide allows. The mail itself stands whole in the conversation for the person to read, and it, not the spoken paraphrase, is the record (3.4). A second mail before the voice has finished speaking is a second answer, appended under the same rule; a mail that is the person's own (they typed meanwhile) never goes back.
5. A correction spoken while the yard works is a new delegation, and its mail follows the first as a typed correction would; the first is not taken back, since a mail sent cannot be. Interrupting speech stops the speaking — footplate drops the Live audio it has queued and not yet played, which is its own bookkeeping since the audio deltas carry no played marker — and leaves the yard's work running, as the guide has it; its answer is still appended when it comes and spoken then, and the model, which heard the interruption, holds it against what the person says next.

Nothing else reaches the voice. Notifications, other yards' mail, other conversations and the timetable are never appended: the session hears the open conversation's answers and nothing else of the yard, and the person reads the rest where they read it today.

### 3.4 What "never invents" promises, as decided

The bead as first asked would have the voice never invent a yard fact at all. OpenAI's prompting guide is plain that the model chooses the wording for ordinary replies and that instructions steer it, so a model told never to guess can still speak a yard fact it was never given, and client delegation, the shape and the append rules constrain only what reaches the model, not what leaves its mouth. Holding the strict rule in speech would take a gate at the speaker that buffered the model's audio, judged each segment's transcript and played almost nothing in the model's own voice, with footplate reading the yard's mail aloud itself in a second speech voice. mckean decided against that (section 5, decision 9): the voice speaks the yard's answers in its own words, in one natural voice, and the design promises only what can be held — the yard's record is never invented. What that means, in the shape, the sends and the prompt, is this.

The record is the mails. The only words that reach the yard are the person's transcript, by the tested rule of step 2 of 3.3, so the model cannot put a request of its own into the yard. What the voice is meant to speak from is the counterpart's mail as footplate appends it during the session, which stands written, whole, in the conversation beside the captions. The model is not bound to that source: it may answer directly without delegating, or state a yard fact it was never given in this session, in wording of its own — the delegation guide has what footplate appends spoken paraphrased, and the prompting guide has instructions steer the model rather than guarantee its speech. A spoken answer may therefore be wrong in more than paraphrase — an added, dropped or misstated fact, or one never given at all — and the plain line of 3.1 says so before anyone turns voice on, with the written mail as the record to read against it. There is no gate, no second speech path and no discarded speech to measure: model audio goes to the speaker as it comes, and interruption drops what is queued (3.3, step 5).

The shape and the prompt still steer what can be steered. Client delegation gives the model no tools and no backend but footplate, and footplate appends nothing as fact that did not arrive from the yard during the session — answers by `commentary.append` to be spoken, and by `thinking.append`, unspoken, progress with no fact in it ("lookup in progress" is the guide's own example) and the ask direction of 3.5. Responses delegation, where OpenAI's model would be the backend reasoning over the yard, is not used. The session's instructions, a constant in the voice module held by a test to the words that carry the rule, say in the prompting guide's shape:

```
You are the voice of footplate, the window onto this yard. Speak plainly
and briefly. While you wait for the yard, say "let me check" or "let me
think"; never say you will ask anyone, and never name the machinery. If
a decision waits in the window, take no answer to it; tell the person
it waits there. The person is talking with {counterpart} in the yard;
everything that needs the yard is delegated, and footplate sends it as
their message and brings the answer back for you to speak. Speak each
answer briefly in your own words from what footplate gives you; the
written mail in the window is the record, so add nothing to an answer
that is not in what you were given.

Delegation policy:
Backend tools:
- The open conversation: ask {counterpart}, and hear their answer.

Generate <|SpawnThinking|> when:
- The person asks anything of the yard, of {counterpart}, or that needs
  a fact you have not already been given in this session.
- A correction changes what was already asked.
- The person asks to hear an answer again; footplate gives it again.

Do not generate <|SpawnThinking|> when:
- The person greets you.
- You need a brief clarification before the request makes sense.

Generate <|SpawnThinking|> before giving an answer that depends on the
yard's work. Do not guess the result while waiting, and do not state a
yard fact you have not been given in this session.
```

`{counterpart}` is the open row's name as the list shows it. Where the model speaks a yard fact wrongly anyway, the written mail is untouched: the yard holds only the person's mail and its own answers, and the person who acts on a spoken name, number or state without reading it acts on spoken words the design has already warned may differ from the mail or have no mail behind them at all. That warning, and one natural voice with nothing buffered or discarded, is the price taken knowingly at `decide`; the strict gate remains describable from the first draft's history if a later bead wants it, at the delay and second voice it costs.

### 3.5 While the yard works, and the asks

While a delegation waits, the voice says the natural thing and not the machinery: never "I'll ask", never the backend, delegation or the yard's calls. `delegation.acknowledgement_delay_ms` is set at 800, the guide's figure, as a preference for quiet on a quick answer: the session reference allows the model to speak before the delay and 800 is its maximum, so a quick answer may still meet a filler, and any thinking or commentary append cancels a pending acknowledgement; the spikes watch for both (section 6, beads 1 and 2). A slower wait is met first by the model's automatic acknowledgement, which the instructions above steer to words such as "let me check" and "let me think", spoken as it comes while the window's state words carry the wait. Where the yard works longer — the counterpart's state in the model says working, which the crew list already reads — footplate sends no invented progress speech: one `thinking.append` of the progress kind when the wait passes half a minute, so a later answer still makes sense, and the window's state words say `yardmaster is working` where the person can see them. When the answer comes the voice speaks it (3.3 step 4); where the yard refuses the mail or the yard is away, the refusal's own words, which are the yard's, are what footplate gives the voice to speak, once.

Asks are not answered by voice in the first version, and that line is a first-version line, not a lasting rule: a later bead may add voice answers with safeguards of its own, and only a new decision would need to reopen this one if it were ever made lasting (section 5). The voice module holds no path to `AnswerAsk`, `ApproveAsk`, `ApproveSetup` or `GiveSecret`: it cannot name them, and the calls stay behind the cards' taps, keys and weighty gates exactly as today. Where an ask arrives in the voiced conversation while a session stands — the model knows, as it raises the card — footplate appends one direction by `thinking.append`, in words with no ask content beyond its standing, so the model takes no answer to it and tells the person in its own words that a decision waits in the window and needs a tap there, carrying no name and nothing of the question or its options. A person who answers by voice is told, in the session's instructions' words, to tap it in the window. A secret ask is not named even so far: nothing of it, not its bead nor its names, is appended and the voice says nothing either, since what is read aloud goes to the provider; the card waits in silence where it waits today. What the person says instead — a fresh request about the bead — is handed on as an ordinary mail, as `yardr chat` would carry it.

### 3.6 What the person sees, in one exchange

The settings' Voice section, as typed and seen: a switch at off; tapping it asks for the key in its masked field under the plain line of 3.1, and with a key kept the switch stands on and the section says `voice is on`. In the conversation with yardmaster, the head gains `Voice`; tapping it, the button says `listening` and the caption line stands empty. Then:

> Person (spoken, as the caption shows it): *What's blocking the release?*
>
> Voice: "Let me check." — and the caption gives the words to the mail that appears under them in the conversation, from the person: *What's blocking the release?*
>
> (yardmaster's row state reads working; the head says `yardmaster is working`.)
>
> Voice, on yardmaster's mail arriving, speaking it in its own words: "Everything else has landed — the Windows zip is still building." — words of its own over the mail in the conversation, which stands there as written and is the record if the two differ.
>
> Person, over the voice's last words: "Tell them to land without it if it's not done by four." The speaking stops, the words caption, delegate and go as a second mail; the first answer is not repeated.

Ending the session leaves the conversation exactly as if the mails had been typed: there is no voice record to keep, export or delete.

### 3.7 Endings and failures

A session that cannot be made — no key, a key OpenAI refuses, no network — says why beside the button in the yard's own manner ("OpenAI answered 401" is a refusal's words, shown, never parsed) and leaves the session unmade, never retrying unasked. The provider closing the session mid-conversation (its reference gives `expired` for the session duration limit and `session.started` carries `expires_at`, though the number is nowhere stated, so footplate reads the expiry when the session starts and expects it) ends it as a hang-up does: the caption clears, the button says `Voice` again, and nothing is re-seeded or resumed silently; a new tap starts a new session knowing nothing of what went before. The yard going away mid-wait is 3.3's refusal: the delegation closes, the voice says the yard does not answer, and the mail is not resent when the yard returns. A session left standing with nobody speaking closes itself after the idle limit (section 5, decision 6), since Live bills by the second; one a person ends closes at once. Logs (`src/log.rs`) take the session's openings, endings and refusals as they take the window's other sayings, without transcript or mail text.

## 4. Left out

**Phones, and Windows voice in the first build.** mckean settled desktop only (note 17782), which settles where the key stands and ends the question of where a phone would keep one; a phone is not this footplate in any case (README.md, Feedback). Windows footplate is a desktop window too, but its microphone consent, its audio path and a gate that runs build and test alone there (`.github/workflows/windows.yml`) make it a follow-on, not a first build (section 5, decision 7): the hand-off, settings and key beads are written so nothing in them is macOS or Linux but the audio calls behind `cfg`.

**Answering asks by voice in the first version.** No picking an option, no approving, no declining, no secret by speech in the first version — no more than that is ruled out: the bead asks it settled for the first version, mckean confirmed the line is for the first version only (section 5), and a later bead may add voice answers with safeguards of its own. The weighty gate (the unlock, the typed-back number) is a hand's proof that speech, which another person in the room could supply, should not stand in for without such safeguards (3.5). Reading an ask's question and options aloud is left out with it in the first version: what is read aloud goes to the provider, and an ask's context can carry what a mail would not.

**Responses delegation, native Decisions delegation and a Realtime build.** Responses delegation would make OpenAI's model the backend that reasons and answers, with footplate reviewing words already shaped by another model's reading of the yard: footplate would no longer be the backend handing work to the yard. Native Decisions delegation (section 2) answers defined choices and a whether-to-call-Responses question; it cannot carry a free-form spoken request on as mail. A Realtime session would reason and call tools itself, so footplate would have to give it the yard as function tools and trust its tool calls; each is more reasoning between the person and the yard than handing the message on, at more wiring. The chained pipeline (speech-to-text, a text model, text-to-speech) shows the text between stages, which this design does by sending mail, without a text model at all. All stay possible behind the voice module if Live disappoints (section 5, decision 1); none is proposed.

**A gate at the speaker, a second speech voice, and a judge of the voice's words.** The strict holding of "never invents" — buffering the model's audio, judging each segment against a closed list and footplate reading the yard's mail aloud itself — is left out because mckean chose the natural voice over it (section 5, decision 9, section 3.4): its delay, its discarded clarifications and its second voice are not paid. A second model asked whether a paraphrase is faithful to the yard's text is left out with them, at a second model's cost and delay on every answer and with a judge that can itself be wrong. The safeguard that stands instead is the written mail beside the spoken words, already in the conversation; no check of spoken speech after the fact is kept, since the record needs none.

**Speaking anything but the open conversation's answers.** No notifications spoken, no other yard's or conversation's mail, no timetable, no bead notes by voice (`AddNote` stays typed on its card), no files by voice in either direction (attachments stay in the draft), and no session while the window is shut, hidden or minimized — hiding or minimizing the window ends a standing session and stops the microphone: the tray and menu-bar following goes on notifying as today, silently. Voice starts only by a tap in the open window and ends when that window is no longer showing.

**Keeping a voice record.** No transcript file kept by footplate, and `store` false at the provider, so no saved session recording is kept to download or fork from; microphone audio streams to OpenAI while the session stands, and what was said may appear in OpenAI's abuse-monitoring logs, retained for up to 30 days by default (the data-controls page for `/v1/live/sessions`, https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data), which the plain line of 3.1 names. Captions are gone with the session (3.2). The mails are the record, kept by the yard as any mail is; a person who wants the spoken words kept has them, as mail.

**A chosen voice, a wake word, and push-to-talk.** One session, started and ended by its button, in the provider's default voice (section 5, decision 8). The model is full-duplex and hears while it speaks; a wake word is a listener standing open before the opt-in's session, which the off-by-default switch forbids in spirit; holding a key to talk fights the natural interruptions the bead asks for. Headsets against echo are recommended in the section's help line, not enforced: the spike (section 6, bead 1) says whether laptop speakers pass.

**Proactive speech.** The voice never starts speaking unasked: a mail that arrives with no delegation waiting (section 5, decision 4) and a crew member's state changing are shown, not spoken. The yard works for minutes at a time; a voice that reports on its own would talk over the person.

## 5. Open questions, now decided

The first draft left the ten questions below to the operator at `decide`, each with its options, its costs and the answer it recommended; the proposal was drawn as those answers would have it. They are all decided now, and this section records each decision with who took it, so the document can be read without the bead's notes. Nothing here is left open, and section 3 above is drawn as decided; a later change to any line below is a new decision, not a reading of this one. mckean decided questions 1 and 9 and the scope of the ask line; the yardmaster decided the rest under mem-gres, as the draft recommended, and mckean may overrule any of them (notes 17820, 17824, 17828).

1. **Build on GPT-Live. Decided by mckean (ask 17793).** GPT-Live in client delegation, as drawn in section 3: the only offer whose shape hands work to the application's own backend, with spoken and unspoken appends and the acknowledgement delay. It costs $0.05 a minute billed per second; in client mode there is no OpenAI-managed Responses backend charge, since the backend is footplate and the yard, while any costs the yard itself incurs in doing the work remain the yard's, separate from the session's bill. Its standing is settled in section 2: generally available since 10 Sep by OpenAI's changelog. What remains against it is narrower: its key has no short-lived form, so the desktop itself is the trusted side, and the numeric session limit is unstated (each session is told its own `expires_at`) — borne by a first build whose provider stands behind one module (section 6, bead 4), so leaving Live later is one module, not the window. The Realtime API and waiting were both set aside: Realtime would have footplate write and guard a tool surface and hold a reasoning model to its tool results, and waiting would only have slept behind the launch rule. The spikes' go or no-go (beads 1 and 2) is still read before the window is touched.
2. **A new audio dependency is acceptable in principle; the spikes judge it. Decided by the yardmaster (note 17820).** footplate has no audio path, and gpui offers none. A maintained cross-platform capture and playback crate may be taken where the spikes find one that passes — mono PCM16 at 24 kHz both ways on macOS and Linux, playback that can be dropped mid-queue, no C library the release scripts must learn to ship (the tree chooses pure-Rust where it can, and `scripts/release.sh` lists every archive member), and a licence the NOTICE file can carry — and the systems' own calls behind `cfg` (the `objc2` and CoreAudio family the tree already binds on the Mac, ALSA or PipeWire on Linux) stand where none does. The system's dictation reaches neither the continuous stream nor the droppable queue, so it is out either way.
3. **The WebSocket is hand-written. Decided by the yardmaster (note 17820).** Hand-written in the voice module over `api.rs`'s TLS: an HTTP upgrade, then client-masked frames, fragmentation and ping and pong, some hundreds of lines with their tests, in the tree's stated way ("HTTP hand-written, no client library", AGENTS.md) and adding nothing to Cargo.toml. Its risk is a framing fault only a real session shakes out, which the spikes are for; a crate would have been quicker to a speaking session at one more dependency.
4. **Only awaited answers are spoken. Decided by the yardmaster (note 17820).** A crew member may write unasked, or answer a typed mail, while a session stands; such mail is shown in the conversation, and the head says new mail stands there, but it is not spoken. Speaking every counterpart mail was set aside (a voice that talks over the person, at per-minute cost), as was speaking only the first line (an interruption with a cut-off rule of its own).
5. **The key is typed in the settings, with the environment read first. Decided by the yardmaster (note 17820).** Typed in the Voice section's masked field and kept in `voice.key` beside the settings (3.1): a person with no terminal can do it, which is footplate's purpose. `FOOTPLATE_OPENAI_KEY`, where set, is read first and the file is not read. A file-only section (nothing secret ever typed into the window, at the cost of asking a terminal of exactly the person footplate exists to spare one) was set aside.
6. **A session closes itself after five idle minutes. Decided by the yardmaster (note 17820).** After five minutes with nobody speaking and no delegation waiting, and the head counts its minutes and cost meanwhile (3.2), so a forgotten session bills at most 25 cents past its last use; a working conversation, which speaks or waits, never meets the limit. The five is a setting beside the switch. No idle limit (a forgotten session billing until the provider ends it) and a hard length as well (a session cut mid-work, restarted cold) were both set aside. Live's numeric duration limit stays undocumented; each session is told its own `expires_at` (3.7).
7. **macOS and Linux first; Windows after. Decided by the yardmaster (note 17820).** The audio spikes prove two systems, the Mac's microphone consent (`NSMicrophoneUsageDescription`, which `scripts/bundle-mac.sh` must carry) and a Linux desktop's are known quantities, and the Windows job builds and tests but cannot listen. The settings, key and hand-off beads are system-free, so Windows follows by its audio path alone. All three together (a third audio proof and a consent story nobody could hear before it shipped, on the only platform with no yard of its own) was set aside.
8. **The provider's default voice. Decided by the yardmaster (note 17820).** Live makes the voice (`audio.output.voice`, `marin` in the guides' examples) and model at the session's start and changes neither standing; the first version takes the default and keeps no voice setting, so every person hears the same voice while the feature is judged and the new section holds one choice fewer. A short list of documented voices beside the switch (a sitting to record the list, a session restarted to change it) was set aside. Language follows the person's speech either way.
9. **Natural: the voice speaks answers in its own words. Decided by mckean (ask 17821, note 17824).** No gate at the speaker, no second speech path: the model's paraphrase of an answer is played as it comes, and the design promises only that the yard's record is never invented — the person's mail is their own words and the mail in the window is the yard's — while accepting that a spoken paraphrase may state a yard fact wrongly or one never given, corrected only by the written mail beside it (3.4). The strict holding (buffered audio judged segment by segment, only a closed list played in the model's voice, yard answers read word for word by footplate's own speech path, all else discarded unheard) was set aside with its delay, its discarded clarifications and its second voice; section 4 keeps it named, and the spikes no longer measure discards, since there are none.
10. **The first counterpart mail during a wait is taken for the answer. Decided by the yardmaster (note 17820).** Section 3.3, step 4 matches by order because no mail or event names what it answers: the first mail from the counterpart after the spoken mail, matched to the oldest waiting delegation, is appended and spoken as its answer. It is wrong whenever the counterpart writes unasked during the wait, or answers the second of two spoken mails first; a spoken correction follows a misreading as a typed one does, and the mail stands written. Never assuming (every waiting mail shown and spoken only when asked) and assuming only while one delegation waits were both set aside for now; if misreadings prove common, assuming only while one waits is the agreed next step, without new machinery.

Beyond the ten: **asks are unanswered by voice in the first version only. Decided by mckean (ask 17825).** The first version behaves as 3.5 draws either way; what this settles is the wording — the door is left open, and a later bead may add voice answers with safeguards of its own. Making the line lasting (cards always answered on screen, reopenable only by a new decision) was set aside.
