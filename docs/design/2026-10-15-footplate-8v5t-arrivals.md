# Arrivals in footplate: the yard serves the board once, footplate draws it

Design footplate-8v5t, proposed 2026-10-15. A proposal as it was decided on, not a description of what the code does today; the decision is on the bead.

Design for bead footplate-8v5t (flow `design.design`, stage `draft`). A yard is one installation of yardr: its beads, its crew and its depots. A bead is one durable piece of work with a stage; a stage is one step of the flow the bead travels; a depot is where the bead is worked, a git repository or a plain directory. An arrival is a bead still travelling, shown with when it is expected. The platform is the stage a bead stands at; the line is its depot. footplate is the yard's native app, a window onto the yard that talks to it over the socket API. signalbox is a second front end that reads the yard only through the web view's JSON routes and its event stream. The web view is the yard's pages in a browser (`yardr web serve`). See docs/glossary.md in the yardr repository for all of yardr's words.

## 1. The question

mckean, 15 Oct 2026: "maybe there's a good way to show arrivals in footplate as well". signalbox has an arrivals board, the beads in progress as rows, each with its expected arrival time; footplate shows decisions, mail, crew talk and settings, and nothing of the line. The shape to weigh: the yard computes the rows once and serves them as one view over the socket API, footplate draws them as a panel in the yard window with no logic of its own, and signalbox may later read the same view instead of computing. The questions are where the panel sits, whether a row opens the bead's page in the browser, and what the phone-sized window shows.

## 2. What exists today

**Expected arrival is one shared arithmetic already.** Design yardr-hrah (docs/design/2026-10-14-yardr-hrah-bead-eta.md in the yardr repository) gave every bead an ETA as a usual range and a late mark. `Estimate` in internal/eta/eta.go is pure over its inputs: it walks the stages ahead of the bead along its flow's `done` outcomes, sums the stretched claim medians for the low end and the stretched p90s for the high end, adds the simulated queue wait, and marks the bead late when its stage age passes the matching clock's p90. Its package comment says the point outright: "the CLI, the web view and the socket share one arithmetic and one pair of JSON shapes instead of reimplementing them". The store serves it per bead and per response (`Store.Estimate`, `Store.EstimateMany` in internal/store/etaestimate.go), where many beads share one queue snapshot. A stage whose cell holds fewer than 10 samples (`SampleFloor`) is unclocked: the range says so, and a bead with nothing clocked behind it gets no ETA at all.

**The web view's waits are not arrival rows.** `internal/web/waits.go` computes what waits for a person for the `/line` page: held beads, sessions blocked on a prompt, stranded beads, stages routed to a manual group, and open asks, newest first, with parked beads apart and late beads under them (`LateRow`, which does carry the ETA). That is a different set in a different order from an arrivals board: it answers "what needs a person", not "what is travelling and when it arrives". The bead's body says the three readers would share "the same rows the web view's waits and signalbox's board derive today"; checked in the code, the two derive different rows, and what they share is the definition of "expected" in the eta package, not the rows.

**signalbox computes its board from the web view, not from BDP.** The bead's body says the board is "computed in src/board.ts from the bdp feed and the yard's spans"; the first half of that is wrong. signalbox's server script reads the web view's JSON routes (`/beads` and the other lists) and its event stream, and nothing else (signalbox docs/replay.md; the hrah design: "signalbox is a second front end that reads the yard only through the web view's JSON routes and its event stream"). BDP is the Bead Protocol Read profile (internal/bdp in the yardr repository), a separate serving surface for agents; signalbox never touches it. The script projects each bead's `eta` and `late` from the view's `/beads` JSON into yard.json (`beadOf`, `etaOf`, `lateOf` in signalbox src/project.ts), and `rows()` in signalbox src/board.ts turns them into the board: beads in progress (everything but `backlog`), a running session first, then ordered by expected arrival, capped at 15 (`LIMIT`). Ordering re-parses the yard's printed spans (`minutes()`: "2h 5m" to minutes); a bead the yard serves no ETA for sorts after beads with one, then by stage age (`moved_at`, else `created_at`). The time a row shows is the yard's range, one span when both ends agree, "—" (`NO_TIME`) where the yard serves none (`etaText`). The remark is held, stalled, waiting for a person (`waits for <members>`), late, in that order, else blank; held and stalled are the red tone, waiting and late the amber one (`remark`). bead-1g6c, now live, renamed the board from Departures to Arrivals and changed nothing else.

**footplate's yard window has sections, not tabs.** `list()` in footplate src/window.rs draws the sidebar: a Decisions section (`section_head("Decisions", …)` with the open count in words), a Crew section, then the yard row; the right pane shows the open conversation or, for the yard row, the yard's notices and mail (`talk_yard`). There is no Mail section: mail lives in the conversations and in the yard pane. The bead's "own tab beside Decisions and Mail" therefore reads, in the window as it is, as an own section in the sidebar. The status row is one line by rule: `switch_state` draws a dot, the state word and a quiet Start/Stop/try-again button, and a test pins that it holds at the narrowest window (`the_status_row_holds_at_the_narrowest_window`). The design system says the same ("Status row: a dot, the state word and a quiet action, nothing else"). The window opens at 960 by 640 and goes no narrower than 560 by 360 (src/main.rs); the sidebar is 270 pixels at the usual text size (src/window.rs `column`).

**footplate follows the stream and re-reads its views.** `Client::events` in src/api.rs holds the socket's event stream open (`GET /events?after=`); `Model::event` in src/model.rs turns each event into effects (`Effect::Asks` on `noted`, `Effect::Crew` on `crew_*`, and so on), and the window re-reads the named views. The socket contract it reads against is `Calls` in yardr internal/rpc/api.go, documented in yardr docs/api.md ("A name here is on the wire for good"). src/api.rs mirrors it call for call (`user_asks`, `crew`, `conversation`, and `bead`, which reads one bead with its notes and asks). The bead's body says src/api.rs "has no bead view": what it has no listing of is beads. There is no arrivals-shaped call today: the socket's `Estimate`/`EstimateMany` are yardr's own methods (array arguments, not in `Calls`), for the CLI.

**A row can already open a page in the browser.** A mail's or a note's attached page opens through the web view's address from settings: `Address::page_url` builds the URL, `means.open` opens it (src/window.rs, beside "page open"). The web view serves a page per bead at `/beads/<id>`, so the same mechanism reaches a bead's page; footplate only has to point it at a different path. No native phone footplate is decided: design yardr-xpf8 decided "no native phone footplate yet", and footplate-fwfq (a footplate on a phone) is parked behind the hosted trial.

**The panel kit draws sections the same way.** The footplate-style panel in yardr-design ui_kits/yardr-panel/ is a 340px panel: a header, one status row, then Decisions and Crew under one style of section header (title left, the count in quiet words right: "2 waiting", "all idle"). Tokens live in yardr-design tokens/: one rust accent, green only for the running dot, warn red only for errors. The railway vocabulary rule governs copy: arrivals, platform, line are the words; no generic "tasks" or "dashboard".

## 3. The proposal

The yard serves one arrivals view; footplate draws its rows and nothing else. Two beads, in this order: yardr first, then footplate. signalbox keeps computing its board as today; reading the served view instead is left out (section 4).

**The view the yard serves.** A new computation in the yardr repository, beside the estimator it stands on (where `Store.EstimateMany` lives in internal/store/etaestimate.go, over the same inputs: the beads, routes, groups, cells and one queue snapshot). It answers the open beads that are travelling — everything but the backlog, as the board does — each as one row: the bead id, the title, the platform (stage), the line (depot), the expected arrival as the yard's printed range or "—" where it serves none, and the remark with its tone. The order and the remark are the board's, ported from signalbox src/board.ts so the two cannot drift: running sessions first, then by expected arrival, then by stage age, capped at 15; the remark held, stalled, waiting for a person, late, in that order. The port improves on the original in one place: the board re-parses printed spans to order (`minutes()`), while the yard orders on the durations before they are printed and prints once. "Expected" is exactly the hrah low end: minutes until arrival as `eta.low`, the stretched medians plus the queue wait. Nothing here invents a second definition.

The yard serves it over the socket API as a new documented call, `Arrivals`, in `Calls` with an entry in docs/api.md, so it is served to footplate on the local socket and to a paired device on the listener alike. footplate re-reads it the way it re-reads its other views: on the stream's events that carry a bead (docs/api.md already tells a decisions client to read a shown bead again on every event whose `bead` is that bead; the arrivals view is one call for at most 15 rows, so the whole view is re-read).

**The panel footplate draws.** An Arrivals section in the yard window's sidebar, in the panel kit's style: the section header with the count in quiet words ("3 arriving", "none arriving"), then the rows. A row shows the time in mono, the title, and the platform, line and remark faint; a remark with the red tone draws in the window's warn colour, amber in the accent, as the kit's waiting state does. The rows arrive ordered and remarked; footplate sorts, filters and computes nothing. The section's exact place, what a row does when opened, and the narrow form are the operator's (section 5); the mechanisms each option needs all exist. A section between Decisions and Crew is one more `section_head` and row loop in `list()`; a strip under the status row is a new element under `switch_state`. A row that opens the bead's page reuses the page mechanism (`Address::page_url` through the web view address, `means.open`): the web view already serves `/beads/<id>`. At the narrowest window the sidebar is 270 pixels, so a row is two lines: time and title on the first, platform, line and remark faint on the second.

What a person sees, with three beads travelling: under Decisions, "Arrivals — 3 arriving", then rows like "2h–3h · yardr-bwm2 · review · yardr" and, for the faulted one, "— · footplate-p512 · new · footplate · held" in warn red. Nothing to type; nothing to set.

## 4. Left out

**signalbox reading the served view.** The bead floats it as a later step, and later it stays. signalbox reads the web view's JSON, not the socket; serving the same rows there (a web route over the same computation) or teaching its script the socket is a third bead with its own questions, to file once the two here have landed and the rows have settled.

**The via and the badge.** The board's row carries where the bead goes next (`via`) and who builds it (`badge`: group or type). The served view carries neither: via needs the flow's travel order per bead, and the badge duplicates what the title and remark already say. If the panel wants them, they are fields on the row, not a new view.

**Counts beyond the header, filters, live ticking.** No per-stage counts, no filtering by depot, no countdown that ticks each minute: the view is re-read on stream events, and the ETA spans are coarse ("2h–3h"). Ticking would re-read on a timer for precision the ranges do not have.

**Backlog and closed beads.** The board shows what is travelling; the view serves the same set. The backlog is a different question ("what is queued"), answered by `yardr bead list`, not by arrivals.

## 5. Open questions

**1. Where the panel sits: an own section or a strip under the status row.** An own section between Decisions and Crew costs one more section in `list()`, in the kit's style, beside the two sections that already work that way; at the narrowest window it scrolls with the sidebar, and the status row stays one line as the kit and the window's own test require. A strip under the status row keeps arrivals always visible without scrolling, but it breaks the one-status-row rule ("a dot, the state word and a quiet action, nothing else"), takes a fixed bite of the 360-pixel minimum height, and is a new element with no kit behind it. I would take the own section: it extends what is there, and the mock shows it fits.

**2. Whether a row opens the bead's page in the browser.** Opening costs one click handler per row through the mechanism a mail's page already uses; it needs the web view's address set, and where none is set the row has no Open, exactly as a page row behaves today. Not opening keeps the panel read-only rows, and a person who wants the bead types its id or finds it in the web view. I would take opening: the mechanism exists, the failure mode is already designed, and a board whose rows cannot be boarded is a timetable on the wall.

**3. What the phone-sized window shows.** The same rows at every width cost nothing: the section is one column, and the two-line row already fits the 270-pixel sidebar, so a narrower window changes nothing. A compact phone form (time and title only, remark on open) costs a width-dependent layout for a window that is not decided — design yardr-xpf8 said no native phone footplate yet, and footplate-fwfq is parked. I would take the same rows now and revisit with footplate-fwfq: scoping a phone form before the phone window exists is designing against a guess.

## 6. The beads it would become

In the order they land; the second depends on the first.

**1. yardr: serve the arrivals view over the socket API.** Goal: the yard computes the arrivals rows once and serves them as one documented socket call. Where to look: internal/eta/eta.go and internal/store/etaestimate.go for the estimator and its inputs; signalbox src/board.ts (`rows`, `minutes`, `etaText`, `remark`, `LIMIT`) as the ordering and remark to port, ordering on durations instead of re-parsed spans; internal/web/waits.go (`waits`, `blocked`, `held`) and internal/web/panel.go (`stalled`) for the remark's readers; internal/rpc/api.go (`Calls`) and docs/api.md for the contract. Done when: `Arrivals` is in `Calls`, documented in docs/api.md with its row fields and order, served on the socket and the listener; its rows match the board's order and remark on the same fixtures; the depot's test command passes.

**2. footplate (eyes): draw the arrivals panel.** Goal: the yard window shows the served rows as an Arrivals section, rows and nothing else. Depends on bead 1. Where to look: src/api.rs (the `Client` mirrors `Calls` one for one; add the call beside `user_asks`); src/model.rs (`Model::event` and the `Effect` re-reads; re-read the view on events that carry a bead); src/window.rs (`list()`, `section_head`, the row loop, `means.open` for the row's Open); yardr-design readme.md first, then tokens/ and ui_kits/yardr-panel/ for the section style and the railway words. Done when: the section renders the served rows in the served order with no local sorting or remark logic; a row opens the bead's page where the web view's address is set and has no Open where none is; the narrowest window still holds (the status-row test's window, 560 by 360); the depot's test command passes; the note carries a screenshot of the panel.
