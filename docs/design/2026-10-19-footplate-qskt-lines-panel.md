# The Lines view as a signalling panel, in footplate's kit

Design footplate-qskt, proposed 2026-10-19. A proposal as it was decided on, not a description of what the code does today; the decision is on the bead.

Design for bead footplate-qskt (flow `design.design`, stage `draft`). A yard is one installation of yardr: its beads, its crew and its depots. A bead is one durable piece of work with a stage; a depot is where the bead is worked; a flow is the stages a depot's beads travel, in travel order, each stage routed to a group. footplate is the yard's native app, a window onto one yard that talks to it over the socket API. The timetable mode (`Mode::Timetable` in src/model.rs, labelled "Lines" on the rail) shows the yard's beads still travelling: the arrivals board (the served rows in arrival order), one line (a depot) with its flows, and a bead's details. A lamp is a stage's wait light: fault or turn. A headcode is a bead's short handle (the part after the last `-`, `short_bead` in src/model.rs:4387), as a train describer shows a train's number. The panel is the proposed new drawing of the lines: every flow one straight track on a fixed grid, only the lamps and headcodes changing. See docs/glossary.md in the yardr repository for all of yardr's words.

## 1. The question

mckean, 19 Oct 2026, by mail: an outside review of footplate's Lines (timetable) view, which he likes: "doesn't have to be 1:1 (sometimes the look is a bit ai, so let's stay true to our guidelines but use the concepts and the views (train like))". The review (saved text at /Users/mckean/.yardr/notes/hyperagent-lines-2026-10-19.txt, read whole; it is input, not a spec) proposes treating the Lines view the way a signalling panel treats a railway: the layout is fixed and drawn once, every flow is one straight track, its stages are equal sections, and only state changes — lit sections, and short bead handles sitting on the stage they are at. How does the timetable mode draw the yard's flows as one fixed panel, using the review's concepts, inside the yardr-design kit, at footplate's window floor (616 by 360 with the rail) and at 1200 by 800?

## 2. What exists today

**The timetable mode is a list and a pane, behind the rail.** The rail stands 64 pixels wide (`RAIL` in src/window.rs:128, tiles `RAIL_TILE` 52 with a 10-pixel label under each glyph, `Mode::label` in src/model.rs:390: Crew, Decide, Lines). The window floor is 616 by 360 (src/main.rs:112-113: 560 plus the rail's first 56; the shipped rail stands 64 wide with its labels, inside the same floor). The list is 270 pixels at the usual text size (`column` in src/window.rs:10501-10507, `.w(rems(16.875))`, scaling with the text setting), headed by the status row (`switch_rows`, called from `column`). At the floor the right pane is therefore 282 pixels wide (616 − 64 − 270); at 1200 by 800 it is 866. `list_timetable` (src/window.rs:10796) draws the arrivals row (the yard-wide travelling total), the active lines in the served order, the No line row while beads travel with no line, and the quiet lines (nothing in transit) folded under one Quiet head (`active_line_rows`, `quiet_rows`, `quiet_expanded` in src/model.rs:1921-1939; `toggle_quiet` beside them). The pane is a match over `Selected` (src/model.rs:313-332): `Arrivals` the board, `Line(name)` one depot's flows, `NoLine` a plain list, `Bead(id)` the bead's details (`talk_bead` in src/window.rs:13144, with the quiet back link naming where it came from, `bead_back` in src/model.rs:1865).

**The board groups by platform; the flow pane stacks stages downward.** `talk_arrivals` (src/window.rs:12523) heads the board with the complete travelling total and a "15 of M shown" line where the served rows are capped, then draws `arrival_groups` (src/model.rs:1649): the served rows under their platform, the platforms in the served travel order. Above the groups stands the waits banner (`waits_banner` in src/window.rs:12603): `waits_head` ("N wait on you", src/window.rs:474) and then one row per waiting bead, each with an Open card button — the 55-row takeover the review's section 00 names. Bead footplate-shz0, now in `new`, replaces those rows with the head alone as a quiet link to the decisions mode; it is separate from this design, and nothing here waits for it or repeats it. `talk_flow` (src/window.rs:12734) draws one line as the review found it: each flow a section, each stage a head (lamp dot, name, count; `flow_stage` in src/window.rs:12885, the lamp red for fault and in the accent for turn, nothing where neither burns) with its bead rows beneath it, then an Elsewhere section for the line's beads in no stage of its flows. A bead row everywhere (`served_bead_row` in src/window.rs:12984) is the title, the expected arrival in mono at the row's end, and faint the whole id and the remark in its tone (`remark_color` in src/window.rs:517: red in the warn colour, amber in the accent, `you` first among whom a wait names). Stages never collapse. There is no grid, no track, and no headcode anywhere: a bead is a full row with its whole id faint under its title.

**The yard already serves everything the panel's first version draws.** The socket call `Flows` (`"Flows": {}` in `Calls`, yardr internal/rpc/api.go:47; `Store.Flows` in yardr internal/store/flowsboard.go:94; the contract in yardr docs/api.md under `Flows`) serves the yard-wide travelling total, each depot as a line with its travelling total, its flows (name, the bead type it governs, its source), each flow's stages in travel order (stage, the group a bead would be routed to, whether that group is manual, the lamp: `fault`, `turn` or `none`, and the open beads on the stage), each depot's Elsewhere rows, and the beads travelling with no line. footplate mirrors it one for one (`Flows`, `FlowDepot`, `Flow`, `FlowStage`, `FlowBead` in src/api.rs:268-361; `State.flows` in src/model.rs:76) and re-reads it on the invalidating events docs/api.md names with the call. A flow bead carries its Arrivals row (title, the printed expected range, remark and tone) and `working`: an agent's or a command's session has claimed it, a manual group's claim being a person's turn and no work (`FlowBead` in yardr internal/store/flowsboard.go:77-87). The backlog is not a count field: backlog beads stand served on their flow's `backlog` stage like any other stage's beads (yardr internal/store/flowsboard_test.go:158 pins a backlog bead on its stage), and the travelling totals count only the open beads outside the backlog, so a depot's backlog count today is the beads on its flows' `backlog` stages, each bead on exactly the one flow that governs it.

**Four things the review names are not in the socket's Flows, checked against the yard's code.** (1) A terminal flag on a stage: the web view's flow stage carries it (`Terminal` in yardr internal/web/beads.go:205, from `Flow.Terminal` in yardr internal/store/flows.go:27, `IsTerminal` at :256), but the socket stage (`FlowsStage` in yardr internal/store/flowsboard.go:62-74) has no such field, and neither has footplate's (src/api.rs:323). (2) A group's seats per stage: a group has a limit (`Limit` in `Group`, yardr internal/store/routing.go:30; 0 pauses the group), and `Store.Flows` already reads the live sessions to work out `working`, but the socket stage serves only the group's name — no limit, no count of seats taken. (3) When a bead entered its stage: the store keeps it (`StayStart` on the bead's row, yardr internal/store/beads.go:80-84, set at creation and on every move), but neither the socket bead (`FlowBead`, flowsboard.go:77) nor the web card (which carries only `UpdatedAt`, the last change, yardr internal/web/beads.go:38) serves it. (4) Who works a bead: the socket bead serves only the `working` boolean. The yard's sessions know their bead and group (`Session` in yardr internal/store/routing.go:1696), but the `Crew` call serves a member's name, state and status and no bead (yardr docs/api.md under `Crew`); bead yardr-5bd7 (in backlog) asks exactly for that bead on `Crew`/`GetCrew`, from footplate-rs13's conversation header. A fifth, for the siding stub below: the web stage carries the transitions (`Next` in yardr internal/web/beads.go:203); the socket stage does not, so no socket reader can tell a branch stage from a main-line one.

**The kit allows one accent, and footplate's palette already spends it on waiting.** The yardr-design readme (read whole before this draft, as the bead's `eyes` label asks) rules: exactly one accent, yard rust, and it is the "waiting on you" colour; green only for the running dot; red only for errors; no blue, no teal, no amber as a state colour; counts are quiet words at the right, never badges or stat tiles; targets at least 32 pixels; the railway vocabulary (readme lines 10, 75, 83-86). Its tokens (tokens/colors.css) name the surfaces, the rust scale (`--yard-500` #D3602F), `--signal-green` #4CC38A and `--signal-red` #E5675A, with the light theme opt-in by `data-theme="light"`. footplate's shipped palette (src/theme.rs:171-211) renders that same set of roles in its own shades — the accent is the waiting colour and nothing else (the `accent` field's own words, src/theme.rs:137-142), `warn` is errors, `ok` is the running green, and `crew_dot` (src/theme.rs:114) already draws a working crew member with that green, a blocked one in the accent and a failed one in `warn`, never colour alone because the state stands in words on the row. The review's teal, amber and blue therefore have no tokens to draw with; section 3.3 maps its three states onto these roles instead. The review's platform tag is blue in its Mockup C; footplate's `link` blue (src/theme.rs:147) is for links that open an address, and a platform is not one.

**The waits banner fix is not this design's.** footplate-shz0 (in `new`) makes the banner's head a count that opens Decide and drops its rows. This design's summary line (section 3.2) is the same count in the panel's head; the two must not both ship as separate mechanisms, so the beads in section 6 leave the banner exactly where shz0 puts it and the panel carries no second copy of the list it removes.

## 3. The proposal

The timetable mode keeps its shape — rail, the list, one pane — and the panel is what the arrivals row's pane draws. The list does not change at all: the arrivals row, the active lines, No line and the folded Quiet lines already are the panel's index, and every mode keeping one list and one pane is the zj5f settlement this design does not reopen. What changes is the drawing of the three panes the list already opens: the arrivals pane becomes one fixed panel of tracks (section 3.2), a line's pane becomes that line's stages as columns with cards (section 3.4), and a bead's details stand as they are (section 3.5). The departures board is the arrivals board restyled inside the same arrivals pane (section 3.6), reached by a quiet toggle in its head, so the mode has three zooms and one list: panel, line, bead. Nothing is typed and nothing is set; a person clicks a headcode to open its bead, a line's name in the list or on the panel to open its line, and the bead's back link to return, as today. The mock beside this document (docs/design/2026-10-19-footplate-qskt-lines-panel-mock.html, drawn from the kit's tokens, with its screenshots) shows the panel at 616 by 360 and at 1200 by 800, dark and light, and the line and departures views at 1200 by 800.

### 3.1. The review's eight concepts, decided

| # | Concept | Call | Why, in one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One grid, fixed left column, equal stage widths | Take, adapted | The name column and stage cells are fixed pixels (section 3.2); stages align by their place in travel order, because two flows of one depot serve different stage sets and there is no shared column of names to align by. |
| 2 | Main line straight; decide and siding as a stub below; loops not drawn | Take the straight line and the undrawn loops; the stub waits | The socket serves stages flat and no transitions (section 2), so a stub would be guessed from stage names, and flows name their stages freely. The stub is designed in section 3.2 and built only when the yard serves the transitions. |
| 3 | Beads as headcodes, "+N" on overflow | Take | `short_bead` (src/model.rs:4387) already cuts the handle the panel needs; a cell shows two headcodes and then "+N" in quiet words. |
| 4 | A stage lit only when a bead stands on it; lamp and working decide how | Take, in the kit's roles | Section 3.3: no teal, no new amber; the accent is the turn, `warn` the fault, and working is weight, a filled berth and the running green dot the crew rows already use. |
| 5 | Backlog as a count; quiet lines folded into one row | Take | Backlog beads already stand served on their stage, so the count is drawn, never the chips; quiet tracks fold to one row at the panel's foot, as the list's Quiet already folds their rows. |
| 6 | Zoom: panel, line as columns, bead's details | Take | The three panes exist (`Arrivals`, `Line`, `Bead`); the first two are redrawn, the third stands. |
| 7 | Departures: the arrivals board restyled, ETA first | Adapt | The layout is taken (section 3.6); the name is not — the yard renamed its board from Departures to Arrivals (signalbox bead-1g6c) and footplate's call, row and pane all say arrivals, so the board keeps its name and gains the board's strict columns. |
| 8 | A Marey-style history of where time went | Refuse for this build | It needs stage-entry times served per bead and closed beads' journeys, neither of which Flows carries; it is named as a later bead's ground in section 4, not drawn now. |

### 3.2. The panel

**Geometry is fixed pixels, drawn once.** The panel scrolls inside the arrivals pane: down over the lines, sideways over the stages, and the geography never re-lays itself out for the yard's size — that fixity is the concept being taken. The name column is 120 pixels, fixed: the depot's name in the label type and under it the flow's name and, where the flow governs a bead type, its type, faint (the words `flow_section` already heads a flow with, src/window.rs:12855-12874). The backlog cell is 52 pixels and holds a count and nothing else. Every later stage cell is 92 pixels: two headcodes stand in it one above the other beside the track, and a third bead makes the second line read "+N" (headcodes shown plus the count of the rest, in quiet words, never a badge). A stage cell's head is its stage name in the meta type, faint where the stage is empty and in the text colour where a bead stands on it, with its lamp dot at the head's left where one burns. The terminal stage, once the yard serves which stage it is (bead 1 in section 6), closes its track with a buffer stop: a short vertical bar across the track at the cell's right edge, drawn in the line colour; until then the last stage stands plain and nothing guesses from its place, which is the review's own reason for asking for the flag.

**One track per flow, in the served order.** Each track is the depot and flow of the name cell, then its stages left to right in the order Flows serves them, the track itself a 1-pixel line in the border colour running through the cells at the headcodes' height, with a 4-pixel gap at each stage boundary (the review's insulated joint). A stage handled by people (`human: true`) draws its piece of track dashed and its name followed by "· people" in the meta type — the dashed rail the review asks for, in the words `platform_tip` already uses ("waiting on a person", src/model.rs:1778). A track's stages align with every other track's by place: the first stage cell of every flow stands in one column, the second in the next, because the cells are fixed width; where two flows name different stages at the same place, the names differ and the columns still line up, which is what removes the scattered look the review's section 00 names. Under a depot's last track, only where beads stand there, a stub hangs below the track in the sunken surface: Elsewhere, for the depot's beads in no stage of any of its flows. A second stub joins it once the transitions are served: the branch stages (decide, siding and any stage no `done` path runs straight through) as one stub holding their headcodes. Until then every stage stands on the main line, decide and siding among them, exactly as the flow pane draws them today; the stub is a drawing change over served transitions, not a second view. The beads travelling with no line stand as one track of their own at the foot of the active tracks, its name cell reading "No line" and one wide cell holding their headcodes, because a bead with no depot travels no flow and has no stages to stand between. A depot whose flows could not be read (`FlowDepot.error`, src/api.rs:300) draws its track's name cell and the error in faint words where its stages would stand, as `talk_flow` already heads such a line.

**What the floor shows, honestly.** At 616 by 360 the pane is 282 pixels wide: the name column, the backlog count and the first stage cell stand whole, the second stage cell is cut at the pane's edge, and the rest scroll sideways; three tracks and the folded Quiet row stand down the pane's 360 pixels under its head. At 1200 by 800 the pane is 866 wide: the name column, the backlog cell and seven stage cells stand whole (120 + 52 + 7 × 92 = 816), which covers an eight-stage flow — backlog plus seven stages — without scrolling. The panel is an instrument for reading across the room at the large size and a correct, scrollable one at the floor; no smaller stage cell is proposed, because a headcode cut to fit would stop naming its bead. The mock draws both sizes as they are, the floor frame cut mid-cell on purpose.

**The head is a title and quiet words, and the summary is words.** The pane head stands as the board's head does today: "arrivals" in the semibold type, and under it the travelling total as words ("7 in transit · all lines", the arrivals row's own line). Under that, one summary line in the meta type, counted from the Flows beads footplate already holds: how many are being worked (`working`), how many wait on the person reading (`waiting_on_you` in src/model.rs:1705, complete over Flows where the capped board is not), and how many are held or stalled (the red tone). It reads "3 working · 2 waiting on you · 1 held", each count a quiet word at its place in the line, never a tile and never a badge; "2 waiting on you" is the accent colour, as the kit spends it, and a click on those words switches to the decisions mode by the rail's own mode switch, which is footplate-shz0's mechanism for the same count on the board. The stat-tile row the review draws ("4 being worked", "2 your turn" as tiles) is refused: the kit's counts are words, and the line above is those words. At the head's right stand two quiet buttons, Panel and Departures, the open one in the text colour and the other faint, stepping the pane between section 3.2's panel and section 3.6's board; the choice is the timetable's own, kept where `quiet_expanded` keeps the Quiet fold (a plain boolean on the model, section 3.5), and History is not a third button until section 4's later bead serves what it would draw.

### 3.3. What lit means, without a new colour

A stage is lit only when a bead stands on it: an empty stage's cell stands on the sunken surface, its name faint, no lamp, no headcode. An occupied stage's cell steps up to the raised surface. The bead's state then decides the headcode, one marker set used on the panel, in the line view's cards, on the departures board and in the bead's details, where today each pane colours its own rows:

| State, as served | The headcode and the stage |
|---|---|
| Being worked (`working: true`, no fault on its stage) | The headcode in the text colour, semibold, on the raised surface, with the running green dot (`ok`, the crew dot's green) at its left. Green stays what it already is — the running dot — and working is read from the weight and the filled berth as much as the dot, never colour alone. |
| A person's turn (the stage's lamp is `turn`; the bead's remark waits for a person, tone `amber`) | The headcode in the accent, and the stage's lamp dot in the accent beside its name. The accent is the waiting colour; this is the review's amber, in the one accent the kit has. |
| Fault (the stage's lamp is `fault`; the bead is held, blocked or stranded, tone `red`) | The headcode in `warn`, and the lamp dot in `warn` beside the stage's name. Red stays errors only, and a fault is one. |
| Late (the remark is `late` or `late by …`) | The words stand in the line view and the details as today; on the panel the headcode carries a small `warn` dot at its corner, the review's late dot, and its tooltip names the overrun. Late lights no lamp: the lamp stays the yard's fault-over-turn rule, served. |
| Waiting for a seat (named only once bead 2 serves the seats) | No new mark on the panel; the line view's card says "waits for a seat" in words (section 3.4). A bead with no session and no lamp stands plain in the text colour, regular weight. |

The lamp belongs to the stage and the state to the bead, as served: a stage whose beads mix a fault and a turn burns fault (the yard's rule, flowsboard.go's lamp order), and each headcode still carries its own bead's state, so a working bead on a fault stage reads green-dotted beside a red lamp, and the words in the line view say why. footplate computes none of this: the lamp, the remark, the tone and `working` are the yard's, drawn.

### 3.4. The line view: one flow's stages as columns

A line's pane (from its row in the list, or a track's name cell on the panel) keeps its head — the line's name, its travelling words and its flow count — and draws each flow as the panel's track straightened into columns: the stages in travel order, each column 176 pixels wide and no narrower, scrolling sideways as the panel does, its head the stage's name with the lamp dot, and under the name the group words the served stage carries: the group's name, then "· waiting on a person" where the group is manual, then the seats where bead 2 serves them ("builders · 2 of 3 seats"; a group with no route reads "no route matches", `platform_tip`'s words at src/model.rs:1782). The column head's bottom border is the track: 1 pixel in the border colour, in the lamp's colour where a lamp burns, dashed under a manual stage, so switching between the panel and the line keeps where things are, as the review asks. In each column, one card per bead — the kit's one card kind, the Decision item's shape (raised surface, subtle border, 8-pixel radius, no shadow, no coloured edge): the headcode and the title in the label type, then in the meta type the worker where bead 3 serves one ("worked by builder-2"), how long the bead has stood at its stage where bead 3 serves it ("41 min at new"), its expected arrival in mono, and its remark in its tone; a card whose bead waits on the person reading carries the accent on its remark alone, as every row does today. The backlog column is narrower in content, not in width: its count in the section style, then the titles as plain rows, no cards, and "+N more" in quiet words past the first six, because the backlog is a queue to shape, not work in transit; its beads open their details on a click as any card does. The terminal column, once bead 1 serves the flag, shows the buffer stop in its head and stands empty in the ordinary run of things — a bead entering it closes — and shows no landings: Flows serves open beads only, and today's landings are section 4's later history, not a column invented from closed beads the call does not carry. Elsewhere stands as its own column after the terminal, headed "Elsewhere" with its count, its beads as cards, as the flow pane's Elsewhere section does today. At the floor one column and the next column's edge stand in the 282-pixel pane; at 1200 by 800, four columns and a fifth's edge.

### 3.5. The bead's details, and the model's small change

A bead's details stand exactly as `talk_bead` draws them — title, id, stage and line, "arrives …" with its remark, labels, body, notes, the Open-the-page action where the web view's address is set — opened from a headcode on the panel, a card in the line view, a row on the departures board, Elsewhere or No line, with the back link naming where it came from. The one change: a bead opened from the arrivals pane returns to the view it was opened from, panel or departures, so the back link names that view ("panel" or "departures") where it names "arrivals" today; `bead_back` (src/model.rs:1865) already names the arrivals row for both, and the panel-or-departures choice the head's toggle sets is what tells them apart. That choice is the design's only new state: one boolean beside `quiet_expanded` in the model, set by the toggle, read by the arrivals pane and the back link, kept per yard with the mode as `Place` keeps each mode's open row. No new `Selected` variant, no new call, and no re-read: the panel, the line and the board all draw the `Flows` and `Arrivals` the model already holds, re-read on the events those calls already name.

### 3.6. Departures: the arrivals board, restyled

The board the arrivals pane's Departures toggle shows is today's board with the review's strict columns and without its banner rows: the served rows in the served arrival order, flat, under a column head in the meta type — Expected, Bead, Platform, Line, Remark. Expected stands first in mono, as a departure board's time does; the bead's title follows with its headcode faint after it; the platform is the stage's name as a plain word in the text colour (no blue tag: the kit has no blue state, and footplate's blue is for links); the line is the depot's name, or "no line" where the bead has none; the remark stands last in its tone's colour, with `you` first among whom a wait names, as `remark_color` and `you_first` already draw it. Grouping by platform goes: the board's order is expected arrival, and a grouped board cannot show it, which is the review's point; the platforms in travel order still order the panel's reading of stages, and the served order needs no sorting in footplate, by the arrivals no-logic rule. The cap and its honesty stand: at most 15 rows, with "15 of M shown" in quiet words where more travel, M the Flows travelling total. The waits count stands where footplate-shz0 puts it — one line, "N wait on you", in the accent, opening the decisions mode — and the panel's summary line (section 3.2) is that same count in the panel's head, not a second banner: while shz0 is unlanded the board keeps its rows and this design's departures bead restyles the rows under them, and the two beads are ordered in section 6 so the banner is never drawn twice.

### 3.7. What the yard must still serve

The panel and the departures board build entirely on today's `Flows` and `Arrivals`; the line view's full honesty needs three small additions, each a field on a shape that already exists, each served by the yard so footplate keeps drawing without computing. They are beads 1 to 3 in section 6, in landing order, and the footplate beads degrade honestly without them: no buffer stop, group words without the seats, cards without worker or stage age.

| Field | Where it lives in the yard today | What is missing |
|---|---|---|
| `terminal` on a stage | `Flow.Terminal` and `IsTerminal` (yardr internal/store/flows.go:27, :256); the web stage serves it (internal/web/beads.go:205) | The socket stage (`FlowsStage`, flowsboard.go:62) and footplate's mirror (src/api.rs:323) carry no flag; footplate would otherwise guess the terminal from the last place. |
| Seats per stage: the group's `limit` and how many are taken | `Group.Limit` (yardr internal/store/routing.go:30; 0 pauses the group); `Store.Flows` already reads the live sessions (flowsboard.go:113) | The socket stage serves the group's name only; "builders · 2 of 3 seats" and a card's "waits for a seat" (a ready bead whose group has no seat free) cannot be drawn. |
| When a bead entered its stage | `StayStart` on the bead's row (yardr internal/store/beads.go:80-84), set at creation and on every move | The socket bead (`FlowBead`, flowsboard.go:77) serves no entry time and no printed stage age; the web card's `UpdatedAt` is the last change of any kind, not the entry. |

Bead yardr-5bd7 asks for the bead on `Crew`/`GetCrew`; this design's bead 3 serves the worker the other way, on the bead, and the two must agree on one naming, not grow two. |

| Closed beads and their journeys (for today's landings and the Marey history) | The event log holds every move; `StayStart` holds only the current stay | Flows serves open beads only. Not in this build's beads. |

## 4. Left out

**The Marey history (the review's concept 8, and its Mockup B age bars).** A history of where time went — stages down the side, time across, one line per bead, flat stretches for waits — is the honest place for return loops and for "why did this take a day?", and the review itself files it under Later. It needs every stay of a bead, not the current one: stage-entry times are kept for the current stay only (`StayStart`), closed beads leave Flows entirely, and serving journeys is a new call, not a field. Bead 3 serves the current stage's entry, which the history will one day reuse; the history itself is a later design, filed once the panel has settled. The age bar on the line view's cards is left out with it: a bar compares the stage age with that stage's usual time, and no per-stage usual time is served (the ETA's clocks price the journey ahead, not the stage stood in), so a bar would grade one clock by another.

**The stat tiles, and every new colour.** The review's summary tiles and its teal and blue are refused in section 3.2 and 3.3: the kit's counts are quiet words, its accent is rust for waiting, green is the running dot and red is errors. Nothing in this design invents a colour, a badge or a tile to keep.

Open question 1 keeps the choice where it belongs, with the operator.

## 5. Open questions

1. **What the panel's head counts, and whether "waiting on you" there opens Decide.** Option A, as proposed: the summary line counts working, waiting on you and held from Flows, and the waiting words open the decisions mode, sharing footplate-shz0's mechanism and count. It costs the panel a second place the same count stands (the head and, on the departures view, shz0's line), and the two must be kept the same words. Option B: the summary line stands without the link, words only; the panel stays purely for seeing, and a person changes mode by the rail. It costs the one click the review's "open in Decide" promises. I would take A: the count that names work waiting on the reader is the one count in the window that already acts, on the board today, and the panel should not un-teach it.
2. **Whether the siding stub joins this build.** Option A, as proposed: every stage stands on the main line until the yard serves the transitions (`Next` on the socket stage), then the stub is a drawing change in a small footplate bead. It costs the panel its one branch drawing for a while, and decide reads as a main-line stage meanwhile, as it does in today's flow pane. Option B: the yard serves the transitions with bead 1, and the panel draws the stub from its first version. It costs bead 1 a wider shape (every stage's outcomes and next stages, with their contract), and the panel bead a branch layout before the straight panel has been lived with. I would take A: the straight panel is the concept; the stub ornaments it, and the transitions deserve their own contract care.
3. **Whether the line view's backlog column lists titles at all.** Option A, as proposed: the count, the first six titles as plain rows, "+N more". It costs the column a second drawing kind (rows among cards) and a cutoff a person cannot open from the column — a title's click opens that bead, but the seventh title is not there to click. Option B: the count and nothing else, exactly the panel's backlog cell widened; a person who wants the backlog's beads finds them in the panel's cell count and in `yardr bead list`. It costs the zoom its one queue-reading surface, and the review's Line view leads with those titles for a reason. I would take A: the line view is where a queue to shape is read, and six titles with an honest "+N more" say more than a count, at one cutoff.
4. **What the seats words say when there is no seat to count.** Option A, as proposed: a group with no route reads "no route matches" (the words `platform_tip` already serves), a group whose limit is 0 reads "paused" beside its name, and only a routed group with a limit above 0 reads "N of M seats". It costs three wordings where the review draws one. Option B: seats stand only where limit and taken are both served and the limit is above 0, and every other stage says the group's name alone, as today. It costs the one case a person most needs named — a stage nothing can move through because its group is paused or unrouted reads the same as a stage merely quiet. I would take A: the group words exist to say why a bead is not moving, and the paused and unrouted stages are the two that most need saying.
